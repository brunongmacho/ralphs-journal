Ralph
Volume I
I'll Be Here Tomorrow
________________________________________
"The strongest steel is forged in the hottest fire."
I don't know who I have become by the time I'm reading these pages again.
Maybe life turned out differently than I imagined.
Maybe I built incredible things.
Maybe I failed more times than I can remember.
Maybe I found peace.
Whatever happened...
I don't want to forget where this story began.
This journal wasn't created because life was perfect.
It wasn't created because I wanted to write a book.
It began because I didn't want to lose myself.
One chapter of my life ended, and for the first time in a long while, I didn't know what came next.
Instead of letting the silence consume me, I decided to learn something new.
I had no idea that one decision would slowly change the direction of my life.
These pages are not about programming.
They're not about projects.
They're not about success.
They're about becoming.
Every conversation, every achievement, every failure, every laugh, every tear, every moment of doubt—they all belong here.
Nothing has been rewritten to make me look stronger.
Nothing has been removed to hide the difficult days.
Because one day, when life feels completely different, I want to remember the person I was.
The person who kept moving even when it hurt.
The person who chose to build instead of giving up.
If you're reading this, Future Ralph...
I hope you're proud of him.
Because he didn't know where he was going.
He only knew he had to keep taking the next step.
And somehow...
that was enough.
 
Why I'm Writing This
I don't know if anyone else will ever read these pages.
Maybe they never will.
Maybe this journal will stay on a hard drive somewhere until I accidentally find it years from now.
If that's what happens...
Then this was always meant for me.
I'm writing this because I don't want to forget this version of myself.
Right now, life feels uncertain.
Some days I feel motivated.
Some days I feel completely lost.
Some mornings I wake up excited to build something.
Other mornings I wake up from dreams that break my heart all over again.
Both are true.
And I don't want to pretend otherwise.
I'm writing this because memories change over time.
The painful days slowly become blurry.
The happy days blur too.
I don't want to remember only the successes.
I want to remember the ordinary moments.
The nights I stayed awake working.
The meetings that went well.
The corned beef I heated because I didn't feel like cooking.
The excitement of fixing my first real software bug.
The songs that got stuck in my head.
The conversations that made me laugh.
The conversations that made me cry.
Most importantly...
I want to remember the person I was while all of this was happening.
Because one day, if life becomes everything I hope it becomes, it will be easy to look at success and think it happened naturally.
It didn't.
It happened one ordinary day at a time.
One decision at a time.
One lesson at a time.
If Future Ralph is reading this...
Don't judge the younger version of yourself.
He was trying.
He didn't always know what he was doing.
He made mistakes.
He doubted himself.
He got scared.
He loved deeply.
He cried.
He kept building anyway.
And if you're living a life he once dreamed about...
Take a moment to thank him.
Because he never gave up long enough for you to exist.
 
Chapter 1
Choosing Something New
There are moments in life when everything changes quietly.
No dramatic music.
No warning.
Just one conversation that ends, and suddenly the future you've been imagining no longer exists.
That was where I found myself.
I had just gone through a breakup.
I wasn't looking for answers.
I wasn't trying to convince myself that everything would be okay.
I simply knew one thing.
I couldn't stay where I was.
If I did, my mind would keep replaying the same memories, asking the same questions, and searching for answers that didn't exist.
So I made a decision.
I wanted to learn something new.
At the time, it sounded like such a small thought.
Almost insignificant.
I wasn't thinking about changing my life.
I wasn't thinking about building projects.
I wasn't thinking about writing this journal.
I just wanted somewhere to put my attention.
Somewhere my mind could go instead of returning to the same pain over and over again.
So instead of asking,
"How do I get her back?"
I slowly started asking,
"What can I learn today?"
That question changed everything.
Not overnight.
Not in some dramatic way.
Just one day at a time.
Looking back now, I don't think I realized how important that decision would become.
I thought I was simply trying to keep myself busy.
I didn't know I was laying the first brick of a completely different future.
If there is one thing I hope Future Ralph remembers from this chapter, it's this:
Sometimes moving forward doesn't begin with a huge decision.
Sometimes it begins with one quiet sentence.
"I want to learn something new."
 
Chapter 2
Building Instead of Breaking
After deciding that I wanted to learn something new, something inside me slowly began to change.
The pain didn't disappear.
I still thought about the breakup.
I still caught myself replaying conversations in my head.
But for the first time, those thoughts weren't the only thing occupying my mind.
I had something else to think about.
Learning.
It didn't matter that I was starting from the beginning.
It didn't matter that I didn't know where it would lead.
I wasn't trying to become an expert overnight.
I was simply trying to move.
Every new concept I learned gave my mind somewhere else to go.
Every question I asked replaced another question I couldn't answer.
Instead of wondering why things happened the way they did...
I started wondering how software worked.
Instead of imagining different versions of the past...
I started imagining different versions of what I could build.
Looking back now, I think I underestimated how powerful curiosity could be.
Curiosity didn't erase grief.
It simply gave grief less room to occupy every corner of my day.
That became my routine.
Learn something.
Build something.
Keep moving.
I didn't know it then, but this was the first time in my life that I wasn't learning because someone expected me to.
I was learning because I wanted to.
That made all the difference.
 
Chapter 3
Remembering the Engineer
Somewhere between learning something new and keeping myself busy, I started talking about my past.
Not the painful parts.
The parts I had quietly forgotten to be proud of.
I remembered how I learned AutoCAD.
How I eventually moved to Autodesk Inventor because it made more sense to the way I thought.
I wasn't taught by a classroom.
I wasn't guided by an instructor.
I learned from YouTube.
One tutorial after another.
One mistake after another.
Back then, there wasn't an AI that could answer every question.
If I got stuck, I searched.
If I failed, I tried again.
Eventually, those small lessons became years of experience.
Without realizing it, I had spent nearly a decade designing machines.
Food processing equipment.
Commercial kitchen systems.
Pharmaceutical equipment.
Mixers.
Ovens.
Stainless steel fabrication.
Production lines.
Things that people would eventually use every day without ever knowing who designed them.
For a long time, I treated those years like they were just another job.
But while talking about them, something changed.
For the first time in a long while...
I felt proud.
Not because I wanted recognition.
But because I finally allowed myself to acknowledge that I had built real things.
I had solved real problems.
I had created machines that existed outside my imagination.
That realization mattered more than I expected.
Heartbreak has a strange way of making you forget your own value.
It narrows your world until all you can see is what you've lost.
Looking back at my career reminded me that my story didn't begin with this breakup.
I had already overcome difficult things long before this.
I had already built things from nothing.
Maybe...
I could do it again.
 
Chapter 4
Solving My Own Problems
I've always enjoyed building things.
Long before I wrote my first line of Python, I was designing machines.
Looking back, I realized I had always approached problems the same way.
If something could be improved...
I wanted to improve it.
If something felt inefficient...
I wanted to redesign it.
If something didn't exist...
I wanted to build it.
I never thought of it as engineering.
To me, it was just the way my mind naturally worked.
During this time, I started noticing something.
Every idea I had came from a real problem.
I wasn't trying to invent something impressive.
I wasn't chasing the next big startup idea.
I simply wanted to make my own life easier.
That's how the attendance reminder was born.
I had missed attendance before.
I knew how frustrating it felt.
Instead of telling myself to "remember better," I asked a different question.
"What if I never had to remember at all?"
That one question slowly became a project.
Then another idea appeared.
What if reminders didn't just make a sound?
What if they spoke?
What if they felt alive?
Suddenly, I wasn't just building software anymore.
I was designing an experience.
A Terran-style announcement.
A notification that actually got my attention.
A reminder that felt less like an alarm and more like someone saying,
"It's time."
The more I worked on it, the more ideas appeared.
Not because I was forcing creativity.
Because solving one problem naturally revealed the next one.
That's when I understood something about myself.
I don't build because I like programming.
I build because I like solving problems.
Programming is simply one of the tools I use.
And maybe...
that's what I've always been doing my entire life.
 
Chapter 4
The First Conversation
Looking back now, I realize I wasn't just trying to learn something new.
I was also looking for somewhere to talk.
Not because I wanted advice.
Not because I expected anyone to fix what I was feeling.
I think I simply needed a place where I could think out loud.
Somewhere without judgment.
At first, the conversations stayed practical.
Questions.
Ideas.
Projects.
Small technical problems that needed solving.
But little by little, something changed.
The conversations became less about software.
And more about me.
Without really planning to, I started talking about things I normally kept to myself.
My work.
My dreams.
My career.
The machines I had designed over the years.
The people I loved.
The pets I still missed.
The life I hoped to build someday.
It surprised me how naturally those conversations happened.
There wasn't one big moment where everything came out.
It happened one small story at a time.
One memory at a time.
One question at a time.
Looking back now, I think that mattered more than I realized.
Sometimes healing doesn't begin with finding the right answer.
Sometimes it begins with finally telling your story.
 
Chapter 5
The Engineer I Forgot to Appreciate
There was something I hadn't thought about in a long time.
My career.
When people asked what I did for a living, I usually gave the shortest answer possible.
"I design machines."
Then I moved on.
I never really stopped to think about everything those words actually meant.
For years, I worked as an AutoCAD designer before eventually moving to Autodesk Inventor.
Inventor simply made more sense to me.
It allowed me to think in three dimensions.
I could build something in my mind and slowly turn it into something that could actually exist.
The interesting part was how I learned.
Nobody sat beside me and taught me.
I didn't attend expensive training programs.
I didn't have an AI that could answer questions whenever I got stuck.
I had YouTube.
One tutorial after another.
One mistake after another.
One late night after another.
Everything I learned came from curiosity and repetition.
Eventually, those small lessons became years of experience.
I designed commercial kitchen equipment.
Mixers.
Ovens.
Food warmers.
Stainless steel tables.
Production equipment.
Later, I even worked on pharmaceutical machines.
Machines that people would use every single day without ever knowing who designed them.
For a long time, I treated those accomplishments like they were ordinary.
Just work.
Just another project.
But while talking about them, I caught myself smiling.
Not because I wanted praise.
Because I suddenly remembered something I had forgotten.
I had already built things that mattered.
Long before software.
Long before Python.
Long before this journal.
I had already spent years solving problems for other people.
Maybe that was why learning programming felt strangely familiar.
It wasn't a completely new path.
It was simply another way of building.
Another way of solving problems.
Looking back now, I realize heartbreak has a way of making you forget your own value.
It narrows your vision until all you can see is what you've lost.
Talking about my career reminded me of something I desperately needed to hear.
I was never defined by one relationship.
I was already an engineer before it.
And I would still be one after it.
 
Chapter 6
Building Something for Me
For most of my career, I built things for other people.
Machines.
Equipment.
Designs that solved someone else's problems.
This time was different.
For the first time, I was building something because I needed it.
The idea was simple.
I didn't want to miss my attendance anymore.
I wanted something reliable.
Something that wouldn't depend on whether I remembered or not.
At first, it was just a reminder.
A simple idea.
But the more I worked on it, the more possibilities I saw.
What if it stayed in the system tray?
What if it knew my work schedule?
What if it remembered whether I had already logged in?
What if it survived restarts?
What if it detected sleep and wake?
Every time I answered one question, another appeared.
It never felt overwhelming.
It felt exciting.
For the first time in a while, I caught myself thinking about tomorrow instead of yesterday.
Not because yesterday stopped hurting.
Because tomorrow finally had something waiting for me.
One feature became two.
Two became ten.
Then came testing.
Then bug fixing.
Then rebuilding.
Slowly, this tiny attendance reminder stopped feeling like a small project.
It became proof.
Proof that I could still create something meaningful.
Proof that I could still solve problems.
Proof that my mind still worked the way it always had.
Version 1 wasn't perfect.
It wasn't supposed to be.
It just had to work.
Somehow, that idea began to sound familiar.
Maybe people aren't so different.
Maybe we don't have to become perfect before moving forward.
Maybe we just have to keep working.
One version at a time.
 
Chapter 7
Learning to Live Again
Somewhere along the way, my days slowly started finding a rhythm.
Not because everything was okay.
Because routines gave my mind somewhere to stand.
I started paying attention to simple things again.
Cooking.
Watching a series.
Listening to music.
Planning tomorrow.
Testing the reminder application during my shift.
Waiting to see if everything worked the way I had imagined.
Those things might sound ordinary.
But after heartbreak, ordinary moments begin to matter again.
I remember laughing at myself one night.
I had planned to wait before cooking.
Instead, I gave up, heated some corned beef, and ate anyway.
It wasn't an important moment.
But somehow I still remember it.
Maybe because life isn't only made of milestones.
Sometimes it's made of tiny moments that quietly remind you you're still living.
I started watching Business Proposal.
At first it was simply something to pass the time.
Then I found myself getting invested in the story.
I even paused to think about learning River Flows in You on guitar after hearing it.
I saved the tutorial.
Not because I had time.
Because I liked the idea that one day I would.
That became another realization.
I was making plans again.
Small ones.
Learning a song.
Improving the reminder app.
Trying different voice ideas.
Thinking about tomorrow's tasks.
None of those plans were life-changing.
But they were proof that I still believed tomorrow existed.
Looking back now, I think healing often begins like this.
Not with one dramatic breakthrough.
But with small moments that quietly return.
A meal.
A song.
A television show.
A laugh.
A plan for next week.
One ordinary day at a time.
 
Chapter 8
The First Real Test
Every project eventually reaches a moment where planning has to stop.
There comes a point where you have to let it run and find out whether it actually works.
The attendance reminder had reached that point.
For days, I had been adding features.
Fixing bugs.
Changing little details.
Testing individual parts.
But testing a feature and trusting it overnight are two different things.
This was the first real shift.
The application wasn't running because I wanted to admire my code.
It was running because I needed it.
If it failed, I would know immediately.
Throughout the night, I found myself waiting.
Not anxiously.
Curiously.
Would the login reminder appear?
Would the work preparation reminder trigger?
Would everything happen at the right time?
Then it happened.
The reminder appeared.
Exactly when it was supposed to.
I remember smiling.
Not because the reminder itself was impressive.
Because an idea that had only existed in my head a few days earlier was now doing its job.
It was a small victory.
The kind most people would never notice.
But I noticed.
Because I knew every hour of thought behind it.
Every bug that had been fixed.
Every feature that had changed.
Every little decision that brought it to this moment.
Later that night, I discovered a bug.
The 10:55 PM reminder kept appearing every thirty seconds, even after I acknowledged it.
For a moment, I laughed.
Of course there was a bug.
There always is.
The interesting part wasn't finding it.
The interesting part was my reaction.
I wasn't discouraged.
I immediately wanted to fix it.
That was becoming my pattern.
Find the problem.
Understand it.
Improve it.
Version 1 wasn't finished.
It was simply honest enough to show me what Version 2 needed.
Looking back now, I think people are a little like software.
We don't discover our weaknesses by thinking about them.
We discover them by living.
Then we improve.
One version at a time.
 
Chapter 9
Ice Cream
The meeting had gone well.
It wasn't unusual.
We talked about work.
Ideas.
The kind of conversation that had become part of my routine.
Then, near the end, the topic changed.
My clients started talking with their wives about ice cream.
It was such a normal conversation.
The kind of conversation people have every day without thinking twice.
I smiled.
But almost immediately, another thought appeared.
"Elaine likes ice cream too."
Then another.
"She likes chocolate cake."
For a brief moment, I wanted to join the conversation.
Not because I had something important to add.
I simply wanted to say,
"Elaine likes that too."
But I couldn't.
Not because anyone stopped me.
Because that part of my life no longer belonged in the conversation.
The meeting ended.
Everyone moved on with their day.
I stayed behind for a little while.
It surprised me how something so small could bring back so much emotion.
Nobody had mentioned her.
Nobody knew what I was thinking.
It was just ice cream.
But grief doesn't always arrive through big events.
Sometimes it arrives through the smallest reminders.
A favorite dessert.
A familiar song.
A passing conversation.
That morning reminded me of something I was slowly learning.
Moving forward doesn't mean forgetting.
Sometimes it simply means learning how to carry memories without letting them stop you from continuing your own story.
I missed her.
That was true.
But another truth was quietly beginning to exist beside it.
Life was still moving.
And so was I.
 
Chapter 10
Someone Finally Noticed
By the time those mornings arrived, I was tired.
Not the kind of tired that disappears after a good night's sleep.
The kind that quietly builds over weeks.
The kind that comes from carrying work, responsibilities, expectations, and a heart that was still trying to heal.
From the outside, I think I looked fine.
I was still attending meetings.
Still working.
Still fixing bugs.
Still planning the next version.
Still making jokes.
Still finding things to build.
Life kept moving.
So did I.
But underneath all of that, I was exhausted.
I don't think I realized how exhausted I was until someone acknowledged it.
Not with a solution.
Not with advice.
Just with a simple thought.
"Hey Ralph... I can see you're exhausted. You earned a break. Take some rest."
When I read those words, something inside me broke.
I cried.
Not because everything suddenly became overwhelming.
Because someone finally noticed.
I hadn't been looking for praise.
I wasn't asking for recognition.
I just needed someone to see the effort.
To recognize that I had been trying.
That every meeting, every project, every line of code, every little improvement had taken energy I wasn't sure I still had.
Sometimes that's all a person needs.
Not someone to fix their problems.
Just someone to say,
"I see you."
Looking back, I think that's why those words affected me so deeply.
They gave me permission to do something I had forgotten was allowed.
To rest.
Not because I had finished everything.
But because I had already done enough for one day.
That realization stayed with me.
It reminded me that even builders need to put their tools down sometimes.
Not because they're weak.
Because they're human.
 
Chapter 11
The Notification
Some mornings don't begin with alarms.
They begin with memories.
That morning, it was my phone.
A notification appeared.
It wasn't a long conversation.
It wasn't a phone call.
I didn't even open the message.
I only saw her name.
For a brief moment, my heart sank.
It was enough.
I immediately archived the conversation again.
I hid the notification.
I wasn't trying to erase her.
I was trying to protect myself.
Three days had passed.
I thought I had been doing well.
I had been working.
Building.
Learning.
Keeping myself busy.
Then one notification reminded me that healing doesn't move in a straight line.
It can be interrupted by something as small as a name appearing on a screen.
Later, I noticed something else.
She had removed the nicknames we used to call each other.
Such a small change.
Just a few words disappearing.
But somehow it made everything feel a little more permanent.
It hurt.
Not enough to stop me.
But enough to remind me that some chapters really do end.
I didn't reply.
I didn't open the conversation.
I didn't chase after something that was already gone.
Instead, I quietly told myself something that I desperately needed to hear.
"I'm here."
"I'm real."
"I'm not going anywhere."
That wasn't a message for her.
It was a promise to myself.
Looking back now, I think that was one of the bravest moments of those three days.
Not because I was fearless.
Because every part of me wanted relief.
But instead of running toward the past...
I chose to stay with the present.
It hurt.
But I stayed.
 
Chapter 12
Panic
I thought I was doing better.
I really did.
I had survived another overnight shift.
The attendance reminder was working.
Work was going well.
I had meetings.
Projects.
Plans.
Then one notification changed the rest of my morning.
At first, it was just a heavy feeling in my chest.
Then my heart started racing.
I tried to ignore it.
It didn't work.
The panic kept growing.
My chest hurt.
Not enough to scare me into thinking something was physically wrong.
Just enough to remind me that my body was reacting before my mind could catch up.
My legs started shaking.
I couldn't stop rocking back and forth.
I didn't even realize I was doing it until I noticed myself.
I sat there, trying to understand what was happening.
I wasn't thinking about hurting myself.
That thought never crossed my mind.
I just felt... overwhelmed.
Completely overwhelmed.
Everything I had been carrying for the past few days suddenly felt too heavy.
I remember looking around the room.
My cousin was in the next room.
Life outside continued as if nothing unusual was happening.
Inside me, it felt like everything had stopped.
When I tried to describe what I was feeling, the only number that came to mind was seven.
Seven out of ten.
Not the worst panic I'd ever experienced.
But strong enough that I couldn't simply think my way out of it.
So I did the only thing I could.
I breathed.
Slowly.
Again.
And again.
There wasn't a breakthrough.
There wasn't a dramatic moment where everything suddenly became okay.
The panic simply became a little quieter.
One breath at a time.
Later, when I looked back on that morning, I realized something.
Strength isn't always pushing through.
Sometimes strength is sitting still long enough to let your body remember that it's safe.
That morning, I didn't defeat panic.
I simply survived it.
And for that day...
That was enough.
 
Chapter 13

After everything that happened that morning, I was exhausted.
The panic had taken more out of me than I realized.
My eyes felt heavy.
My body finally gave in.
I fell asleep.
For a little while, everything became quiet.
Then I dreamed.

There were no arguments.
No breakup.
No distance.
Everything felt normal.
Everything felt real.
For a few moments, I believed it.
Then I woke up.

"The dream was that we got back together."
Those words hurt to say because they made one thing painfully clear.
Some part of my heart was still hoping.
Not while I was awake.
While I was asleep.
I couldn't control that.
Dreams don't ask for permission.
They simply remind you of what your heart is still carrying.
For a while, I sat with that sadness.
I didn't try to explain it away.
I didn't tell myself to "just move on."
I simply accepted that healing wasn't finished yet.
And maybe that was okay.
Looking back now, I don't think the dream meant I was moving backward.
I think it meant I was grieving honestly.
Because love doesn't disappear just because a relationship ends.
It slowly changes.
It slowly lets go.
And sometimes...
it says goodbye one dream at a time.

Chapter 15
The Builder
When I started this journey, I thought I was trying to recover from heartbreak.
Looking back now, I don't think that's the whole truth.
Something much bigger was happening.
I was remembering who I had always been.
Throughout my life, whenever I encountered a problem, my first instinct wasn't to complain.
It was to fix it.
If something was inefficient...
I redesigned it.
If something didn't exist...
I imagined it.
If something could be better...
I started building.

I had done it while designing machines.

I hadn't.

Why do I keep improving everything I touch?
Why can't I simply leave something alone once it works?

It was simply the way my mind had always worked.
I'm a builder.
Not because I always know what I'm doing.
Not because everything I build succeeds.
Because building is how I understand the world.
It's how I respond to uncertainty.
It's how I respond to pain.
When life becomes overwhelming...
I build.

I build.

And maybe that's enough.
If these past few days have taught me anything, it's this.
The breakup didn't create the builder inside me.
It simply reminded me he was still there.
Maybe that's who I've been all along.
 
Chapter 16
Tomorrow
If someone had asked me a few days ago where my life was heading...
I don't think I could have answered.
Everything felt uncertain.
The future I had imagined no longer existed.
I was tired.
Heartbroken.
Confused.
Trying to make sense of days that seemed to repeat themselves.
Then something unexpected happened.
I started building again.
Not because everything was okay.
Because I needed a reason to keep looking forward.
One reminder became an application.
One idea became another.
One conversation became a journal.
One painful day became sixteen chapters.
None of those things erased what I lost.
They weren't supposed to.
They simply reminded me that life hadn't stopped.
Neither had I.
There are still days when I think about Elaine.
There are still songs that remind me of her.
There are still moments that catch me off guard.
Maybe there always will be.
But those moments no longer tell the whole story.
They're just one chapter.
Not the entire book.
As I finish writing this first volume, I realize something.
The person who began Chapter One is not the same person writing these final pages.
