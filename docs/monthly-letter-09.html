A Letter for September

✦

Dear Future Ralph,

If June was the month I learned to be alone with myself,

July was the month everyone else arrived,

and August was the month I learned how to be home,

September was the month I learned to count.

The country spent the whole month counting down to Christmas.

I wasn't counting anything.

Then my left ear started ringing, and I started.

✦

On the tenth, they gave me a Notice to Explain.

That's how the month started. Not a firing. A question about my work, in writing, with dates on it.

The next morning I logged in anyway and kept the night moving. Ragnarok in one window, resumes in another, Thai food at midnight, because the day after a failure should still have plans in it.

The bench wasn't empty. It was just quiet.

Then, on the sixteenth, at 10:55 in the morning, the answer came.

Not the answer I'd been carrying in my chest for a week.

A written warning.

"the management has decided that your actions warrant a WRITTEN WARNING."

They held the door open a crack and told me to come back in.

A written warning was the best news I'd gotten in a week.

And I used it to remember the number I should have been saying all along.

"I'm worth more than I'm being paid for right now."

Not hoping it. Saying it.

The month took the job off the table and handed me back my own number.

✦

Then the month showed me what the number was actually worth.

Five hundred pesos, until October 2.

I did the math in the first week and then I stopped doing the math.

No ulam, so a sandwich again. Boba on the way home, a hundred forty pesos of it. The bus to Cavite, because the walk cost too much that week.

I remember being embarrassed by how small the choices got, and then eating all of them anyway.

There is a kind of honesty in a day where every peso has a name.

I knew what the day cost before the day started.

Scarcity didn't make me small. It only took the padding off.

✦

Then October became December, and that was the month doing me the biggest favor.

We had an October. Then the October didn't go, and instead of pretending it still might, we gave it a month that could actually hold it.

Her thirtieth birthday is December 31.

There's a table in it now. She cooks, I cook the bicol express. A beach at sunrise or sunset, a sale on bookings I'm supposed to be watching for.

And then I sent her a song.

She wrote back: "And I will treasure this song just as how u remember me with this song"

I didn't write that. I only remembered her inside a song, and she told me she'd hold it.

A maybe became a date. That's what September taught me.

✦

The country had a loud week, and none of it reached me.

On the twenty-fourth the government blocked Discord. Two weeks for the telecoms to open a local office. Everybody had something to say about it.

I checked the machine anyway. DNS already set, DoH already on, the domain still resolving. I flushed nothing, because nothing needed it.

Then I looked at my own account, and the crisis of the morning was a sticker on my avatar.

"it really didnt affect us unless you are not a techy savvy person"

Ten pieces of kwek kwek after that. My mouth burning.

The week that was supposed to be the loudest one turned out to be one of the quietest.

The world did what it does. It stayed on the other side of the glass.

✦

Then my own hours moved without asking me.

The graveyard was eleven to seven for years. I logged out at 7:19 in the morning and nothing happened. No ceremony. One shift ended and I never got a vote on the next one.

Monday I start at eight and I finish at four.

The part I don't like is that I took the new hours to do the same work for someone new, and lost the hours she lives in.

I told her I'd go looking for the nights again after this one.

Then, four days in, the news came that it would move again. Seven to three, once Australia turns its clocks in October. The app that remembers my hours learned daylight saving on top.

In one week the clock moved twice.

So I stopped treating the hours like something I own. The schedule changed. It didn't break anything. December still had a place at the table.

✦

Two days before the new hours, my left ear went quiet and stayed quiet.

Not quiet. Ringing.

The clinic called it profound. I don't like the word, but it's the word on the result, so it's the word I use.

Sudden loss on the left side. The steroids, Omeprazole before breakfast, and a follow-up on the seventh of October at ten in the morning.

The day I found out, I had a launch meeting at nine and I was standing in a hospital corridor by ten.

"Nakakatakot actually. Lalo eto kasi ung puhunan ko. Pano ko magiging VA na walang nadidinig."

I said that to her. That's how I found out what she is.

Because the person who is supposed to be the useful one cannot say that out loud unless somebody is listening.

So somebody was listening.

✦

The tears came a day late.

At one in the morning, after the medicine and the hopia and the entry was already up. I read the day back in words and it all synced in at once.

"everything that happened just synced in right now."

Then sleep never really came. I sat there until the sun came up, waiting for the shift, and told her about it at half past six.

She logged out at eight. I logged in at eight. Everything that day arrived a beat late except that one.

Then the next day I bought the kit. The medicine, and everything a person needs so the day it matters never has to be about an empty shelf.

And that night the real fear wasn't the ear.

It was the question underneath it. What happens when the person who is supposed to be useful suddenly needs help?

She didn't answer it with advice. She checked whether I'd eaten, whether I'd slept, and she was still the one asking at night.

In every game I have always picked the class nobody drafts first.

"we'll be playing as an asmodian race. and my specialty? support. cleric"

Ragnarok priest. Dragon Nest saint. Dota support.

"funny how i also support real life business in my work"

Stand behind the damage and keep everybody alive.

Nobody at work knew my ear was gone. Nobody asked.

She asked before I told her.

That is the whole September. Not the warning. Not the ban. Not the diagnosis.

One person knew what was actually happening, and she checked on it without needing me to be impressive first.

✦

So this is where I'm leaving you, September.

I came in not counting anything.

I'm leaving with twenty-eight resumes by ten in the morning, a written warning I signed at 10:53, five hundred pesos that lasted until October 2, a date in December instead of a maybe, hours that start at eight, the tablets, a follow-up on the seventh.

And one person who knew all of it before I said it out loud.

The country counted down to Christmas.

I counted what was mine.

That was September.

— Ralph.
