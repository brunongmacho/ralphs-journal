The Songs That Remember You

ₚ·.°.☆•

I don't have to press play to hear them. They play on their own, and she's here. Thirteen of them. I didn't pick them for a playlist. They just kept coming back until I noticed what they were doing.

✦

It started with the diving. Two freedivers. That was one of the first things we learned about each other, before anything else. A freediver like me. She trained her duck dive while I counted thirty sessions and a coach I half-jokingly called nanay. She was so new she asked if she could try a fundive without mastering a duck dive yet. I told her she'd need a diver for a neighbor. "Baka need mo kapitbahay na diver din." "bat hindi patulong akong duck dive hahahhaa."

Then the same shift. Nights, both of us, and suddenly the hours matched. She said it herself. "same tayong shift haahahhaha."

Then everything else. Both of us figuring out independence late, sheltered until our late twenties, thirty. She named it for both of us. "iyaqq kaming mga sheltered and recently lang magiging independent hahahhahaa." I answered that I only started at thirty too. Same goals, same hobbies, same dark humor. Even the same first mask, Taos black. She said it best. "We're kind alike in a way."

The songs came after I knew all that. I think that's why they fit.

✦

The first two were the quiet ones. They came before I had words for it.

Chasing Cars is the feeling before words. "I don't quite know how to say how I feel." That's where it began. Not knowing the name for it yet, just carrying it.

Linger is what it does. "Do you have to let it linger?" It stayed. Days passed, and it stayed. She lingered in everything, whether she meant to or not.

✦

Four in the morning. A movie over. And I said it.

"I'm starting to like you."

She was quiet for a moment.

"nubayan kinakabahan ako."

"Maybe I have not received anything like this in a while."

White Flag was built for that morning. "I'm in love and always will be." No white flag, no surrender. I kept that one to myself for a long time.

We Could Happen is how she answered. I didn't confess with it. She sang it back to me. "And I know we could happen." When the person you're falling for sings that line back, the word is already out.

I'm not loud about things like that. We are the opposite of a flex post. The songs believe the thing out loud. We live it quietly.

✦

Then the asking started. It climbed.

At My Worst is the shy part. "Can I call you baby? Can you be my friend?" He asks softly, in that order. So did I.

Something Just Like This is the small ask. "I'm not lookin' for somebody with some superhuman gifts." No cape, no superpowers. Just somebody like her.

Somebody to Love is when the want finally gets loud. "Can anybody find me somebody to love?" Six composed songs, and then the shout.

I Melt With You is when I knew it was real. "I'll stop the world and melt with you." Two freedivers know what it is when the world goes quiet. We've both been there, underwater, where nothing else exists.

✦

Galway Girl is the joy. In the song, he meets her, they stop to talk, and he takes her hand. "I took her hand and I give her a twirl / And I lost my heart to a Galway girl." She heard me. So we dance.

Sunflower is the promise. "Then you're left in the dust, unless I stuck by ya." That's the whole promise, in one line. I'm not going anywhere.

✦

The miles showed up last, when her own start got closer. Boston is hers. "I think I'll start it over, where no one knows my name." She's building that for herself. I let her go do it. I started over the same way at thirty.

Somewhere Only We Know is the ask. "So why don't we go somewhere only we know?" December. Let me in.

500 Miles is the last word. "Just to be the man who walked a thousand miles to fall down at your door." If it were a thousand miles, I'd walk them.

✦

Not all of it is a thousand miles. Some of it is three minutes that play and suddenly she's here, in the middle of my day.

December is the door. She promised to cook for me. I promised my bicol express back. These thirteen are just the walk to the door.

Maybe the songs take the night off. Maybe I just show up.

— Ralph.
