The Day the Adventure Was the Ear

✦

The new schedule was waiting in my email when I woke. Monday to Friday, eight in the morning to four in the afternoon, weekends off. Today was the first day of it, and the first thing it asked of me was the launch.

I took a bath, ate, rehearsed my script. The closer it got, the truer the line: "im a little nervous meeting my founder actually"

The meeting ran an hour and a half and it went clean. That part of the day ended before ten.

The other plan for the day was my ear. The walk to PCGH I'd decided on the day before. In and out, I told myself.

✦

At eight I was already in the Zoom, waiting. She texted good morning and asked how it felt. I said nervous, and the call cut in the middle, and I sat waiting to rejoin. When it was over I told her the truth:

"It really went well. Happy naman si client. And ang bait nya. Hahaha. Daming chikka eh kaya inabot ng 1hr 30mins"

The calls are daily now. Ten in the morning, Australian time. Eight here, the same hour my new schedule starts. At least for this week, the founder's voice is how my day opens.

The same morning she sent two summaries of the call. In one of them my personal interests were already written down: cooking Filipino food, signature dish Bicol Express, free diving, and experimenting with AI tools. I mentioned them once during the chikka. She wrote them down. Free diving was in there too, the dream that already has a month. The summary even spelled the name of my agency wrong. It said Danvers in one place, Dunverse in another. It's Doneverse. I laughed at that more than I should have.

I'd been nervous about that hour for a week. It turned out to be the lightest part of the day.

✦

Hospital right after the meeting, planned like an errand. PCGH told me to go to the OPD. The OPD had no ENT assigned either. Hirap ng public hospi. So I called a private clinic and went.

The doctor checked both ears. Then:

"Chineck eh. Malinis naman. Walang nakabara. Baka daw sa nerves. Kaya need mag hearing test"

She wrote me a medical certificate for the hours of work I'd missed, and she gave me a week of medicine. Omeprazole, twenty minutes before breakfast. Methylprednisolone, three times a day after food.

Then Megamall, the hearing test at Manila Hearing Aid, and the verdict that came with it:

"my hear loss was sudden and it is in the profound stage. the last sstage of hearing loss. but they said that lets hope the meds will work. but yeah im kinda depressed hearing that."

The right ear was fine. The left one registered nothing. A hearing aid wouldn't even carry it, they said, only a microphone on the left side feeding the right ear.

The day cost around five and a half thousand: the travel, the clinic, the medicine, the test. I'd set aside ten thousand for just in case. The HMO was too slow to fight for, so I'd try to have it reimbursed. She watched all of it and decided to finally get her own HMO.

And the fear had a voice:

"Nakakatakot actually. Lalo eto kasi ung puhunan ko. Pano ko magiging VA na walang nadidinig."

✦

At noon, while I was between clinics, she shared something heavy. The milestones she keeps reading about from abroad, and the support that never came her way the way it came to the others. She said:

"I can’t heal my wounds while I keep staring at it"

I told her we start from different lines. I told her my mother died in 2013 and the support disappeared with her. Baby steps, I said. I believe in you. Then the checkup called, and I had to leave her mid-words to go get checked.

In the late afternoon I got home and went quiet. I cleaned my room because I didn't know what else to do with my hands. The list came out all at once:

"i have diabetes. i have hearing loss. my teeth are not good. so many sickness.."

Then I asked her the fear out loud:

"would you still like me kahit bingi left side ko?"

"It’s not even a question if someone should like you nokaba"

She told me I did what I could today. What's left is to give it time. She said she'd include me in my prayers. I told her goodnight and to sleep well.

Hours later I woke with a stomach ache and the 1 AM dose waiting. The result of the test had arrived by email. I sent it to the doctor. The findings, she said, tomorrow. I couldn't make sense of the graph myself. Close to midnight I told her a small good thing:

"nung ininom ko ung steroids medyo humina ung ringin sa left ear ko. sana good thing un."

Past midnight, with no proper meal to be found anymore, I bought hopia. It was my dinner, so I could take the dose.

The launch went clean, and the summary remembered my bicol express. The errand became the day: a public hospital with no ENT, a private doctor who found nothing blocked, a machine that pinned the left ear in the profound range. She held the phone all the way through, and by midnight the ringing was a little quieter.

I called it an adventure when I left. The clinic had another word for it. Profound. One of them was a measurement. The other one is how I got through the day.

— Ralph.
