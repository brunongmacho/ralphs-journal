The Door I Stopped Leaving Unlocked

ₚ·.°.☆•

It was 1:36 in the morning when I asked myself something I couldn't put down.

If superheroes always save the ones in need...

who saves them?

✦

Not the dramatic rescue.

Not the falling building.

The ordinary lonely part.

The part after the fight is over and the cape comes off.

Who is there for that?

✦

The easy answer is another superhero.

Every story loves a team-up.

But I've started to think the real answer is smaller.

The person who saves the one who saves everyone...

is usually someone who doesn't need them to be a superhero.

Someone who says:

"You don't have to save anyone tonight."

"Sit down."

"Eat something."

"I'm here."

✦

There was a time I opened the door for everyone.

I was a yes person.

I gave chances like they cost nothing.

I assumed good intentions until someone handed me a reason not to.

I didn't keep score.

I didn't have guards up.

I just trusted.

And for a long time, I thought that was strength.

✦

Then came the cheating.

The betrayal.

The people who walked through the open door and took pieces of me with them.

The disappearances.

The ones who chose other paths.

The ones who said things that were supposed to last...

and then quietly didn't mean them anymore.

✦

Each one left the door open a little less.

Not on purpose.

I didn't wake up one day and decide to become guarded.

It happened slowly.

One unlocked door at a time.

✦

And somewhere in all of that, "yes" stopped feeling generous.

It stopped feeling like an invitation.

It started feeling expensive.

Every yes was a risk I had to weigh.

Every trust was something that could be taken.

✦

So now I have a handful of people I genuinely trust.

A handful.

I can count them on one hand and still have fingers left.

Everyone else gets a slower version of me.

The version that waits.

The version that watches.

The version that doesn't say yes easily anymore.

✦

Someone once said the thing about heroes.

You either die a hero...

or you live long enough to see yourself become the villain.

I thought about that for a while.

I don't think I became a villain.

I think I just stopped leaving the door unlocked.

✦

But that's where the question gets uncomfortable.

Because protecting yourself from getting hurt...

and protecting yourself from ever being vulnerable again...

look almost identical from the outside.

Both look like boundaries.

Both look like saying no.

Both look like independence.

✦

I don't always know which one I'm doing.

When I keep someone at arm's length, am I choosing peace?

Or am I just protecting myself from the possibility of being hurt again?

The two feelings are so close I can't always tell them apart.

✦

The real test is what happens when someone safe actually arrives.

Can I slowly let them in?

Can I accept care without looking for the hidden catch?

Can I say something vulnerable...

and survive the possibility they might not answer the way I hoped?

✦

People ask how I'm still open after everything.

I didn't have an answer for a long time.

But I've been thinking about the way I am with her.

I'm not saying yes to everything.

I'm not handing over my trust all at once.

But I'm not making her earn back trust she never broke either.

I say what I feel.

I watch what she does.

I let trust come back the way it's supposed to...

slowly.

✦

Maybe that's the middle ground I've been looking for.

You don't get my trust automatically anymore.

But I won't punish you for what someone else did.

✦

And still, one question keeps sitting there in the dark.

How many good people might I miss...

while I'm waiting for them to prove they're not one of the bad ones?

✦

I don't have the answer yet.

But I think the door isn't locked anymore.

It's just heavier now.

And it takes a little longer to open.

— Ralph.
