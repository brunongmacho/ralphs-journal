A Letter to Hana

ₚ·.°.☆•

Dear you,

There's no label between us yet.

I don't need one to write you this.

I've been learning, over these months, that some things don't need to be named to be real. What you are to me is one of them.

So this isn't a letter that asks you for anything. It doesn't need an answer. It's just me, letting you know that you're part of my days now, exactly as you are.

✦

I went through a whole year thinking I'd forgotten how to talk to people.

Not the polite kind of talking.

The kind where you stop performing.

Somewhere in all those conversations, I realized I hadn't forgotten. I'd just been waiting for a reason to stop being careful. And then there was you, who made being honest feel like the easiest thing in the world.

I don't know when it happened.

I just noticed, one afternoon, that I wasn't reading your messages the way you read a stranger's.

I was reading them like they mattered.

✦

I remember the call.

I almost didn't send "How about I call you? Ok lang?"

There's something strange about asking for a stranger's voice. But you said it was fine.

And then everything I'd been assuming about you still had a tone. A laugh. Pauses.

For weeks I'd been filling those in. Then suddenly there was nothing left to fill in. Just you, reading the same words I'd been reading.

A voice makes a person real.

You were already real. That just made it harder to pretend you weren't.

✦

There's a trust in sharing music.

You hand someone your songs and hope they don't laugh.

Hall & Oates from your shuffle. A song I used to sing at videoke back in college.

You knew it.

That felt like being understood in a language I forgot I spoke.

Then the soundtrip became a movie night. Postmortem. Chips on your side. Snacks on mine. We argued about the albularyo scene like it mattered.

That's what it's like when there's nothing to prove.

Two people sharing a screen and a bag of chips, and somehow it's enough.

✦

I've stopped counting the little things we share.

Same shift. Same sleepy language. The same fruit, guyabano, yours and mine. Good mornings after shifts. "Kain boss." "Eat ur lunch na posxz." Checking that the other one actually slept.

There's a name for people like us. Two night workers who say "ingat" to each other at three in the morning and mean it.

I don't need to use it.

✦

There's one thing I keep thinking about.

You told me things you hadn't told your family yet.

On purpose.

Not because I asked.

Because you decided I was someone told first.

That's the trust I've been wanting my whole life. Not being chosen. Being told first.

I hope you know I don't take that lightly. I'm not going anywhere with it. I'm just holding it carefully, the way you seem to hold the people you let in.

✦

You might come to Manila around October.

Coffee, I asked.

Maybe Binondo, you said.

"Malayo pa naman."

You're right.

There's no bridge yet. So there's nothing to cross.

But I can't pretend the maybe doesn't have a shape now. It has a month. A siomai you already picked. A strategy we agreed on. Single orders, shared at the counter, so we can visit more stalls.

Whether we meet or not, I'm not sorry we talked this long.

That's not a promise. It's just the truth.

✦

And I want some things for you that don't depend on me.

I want your life to keep being yours.

Moalboal.

The sea.

Your writing.

Your soundtrack.

The people you choose to let in.

I want you to keep being told first, and to keep believing you were never meant to save anyone.

None of that needs to have anything to do with me.

I just want you to keep becoming whoever you were already becoming before I showed up.

✦

I don't know what to call you.

Friend. Stranger who stayed. Someone I haven't met yet.

I'm fine not knowing tonight.

I just wanted you to know. You're part of my days now.

And I'm glad.

— Ralph.
