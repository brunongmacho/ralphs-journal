The Woman Who Sent Me Home

ₚ·.°.☆•

I called her Mama Del.

My mom's mom.

She wasn't loud.

She wasn't the kind of person who filled a room with stories.

She was the quiet type.

But when she spoke...

you really needed to listen.

✦

She worked in Hong Kong as an OFW for many years.

So I didn't grow up with her.

Most of my childhood memories of her came from occasional visits and stories my mom would tell.

When she finally came home for good, she moved into our condo with my mom and me.

At first...

I was afraid of her.

She was family, but she still felt like a stranger.

That slowly changed.

Little by little, we became close.

Years later, I even visited her in Hong Kong and stayed in her flat for a while.

Those memories became gifts I never knew I'd treasure this much.

✦

She loved cooking.

Whenever she was in the kitchen, there was almost always vegetables...

and shrimp.

Her ginataang langka was my favorite.

Young jackfruit simmered in coconut milk with shrimp.

I don't know if anyone else's tastes the same.

Maybe it never will.

Whenever she cooked...

I always ate more than I planned to.

✦

Mama Del was a pastor at Jesus Is Lord Church.

Sometimes we'd sit together quietly while she read Bible verses aloud.

She never forced faith onto me.

She simply shared it.

More importantly, she taught me something I've carried ever since.

She told me never to judge people because of their religion.

"The important thing is that you have faith."

I still believe that.

✦

She was already a senior citizen then.

Whenever her pension arrived, she'd celebrate the same way every time.

Sometimes we'd go to Chowking together.

Sometimes she'd bring it home.

She always ordered Beef Wonton.

And Asado Siopao.

Without realizing it...

those eventually became my order too.

Even today, whenever I eat them...

I still think of her.

✦

One Christmas, I brought my first girlfriend home.

For reasons I'll probably never understand...

Mama Del didn't seem to like her.

She hardly said anything.

She just kept quietly watching her from across the room all evening.

Whenever I remember that night now...

I laugh.

Some grandmothers don't need words.

Their eyes do all the talking.

✦

Her health slowly declined.

Diabetes.

Complications.

Dialysis.

Old age.

Eventually...

multiple organ failure.

She passed away only six months after my mom.

Six months.

Sometimes that number still feels impossible.

✦

She was in the hospital for her dialysis.

I stayed with her through it.

Then she looked at me and said something I'll never forget.

"Go home.

Get some rest."

So I did.

I went home.

Some friends came over.

We drank.

The next morning...

my uncle called.

Mama Del's second child.

"Mama Del passed away."

✦

I've written about this pattern before.

The people I loved most seemed to leave while I wasn't there.

My mom.

My grandmother.

Jinro.

For years I carried guilt because of that.

But Mama Del's story feels different.

She didn't simply die while I was away.

She was the one who sent me home.

She chose my last memory of her.

Not machines.

Not suffering.

Not fear.

Kindness.

Maybe she knew.

Maybe she simply wanted me to rest.

I'll never know.

But I do know this...

the last words she ever gave me were an act of love.

✦

After she passed away...

I fell into one of the darkest periods of my life.

Almost every day, I invited someone over to the condo just so the silence wouldn't swallow me.

We drank.

We talked.

Then they'd leave.

And the condo would become painfully quiet again.

Months later...

I bought a dog.

And I named him after the drink.

That's how Jinro entered my life.

She was one of the reasons I drank.

And somehow...

the drink became the name of the dog who would help me survive that loneliness.

✦

There's one Bible verse she read to me that has stayed with me all these years.

John 3:3–6.

About being born again.

"Unless one is born again...

he cannot see the kingdom of God."

I remember her quietly reading those words inside our condo.

I didn't really understand them then.

✦

I think I understand them now.

Being born again isn't about pretending your past never happened.

It isn't about forgetting.

It's about allowing yourself to become someone your past never thought possible.

Mama Del went home to God that night.

But somehow...

she's still teaching me.

Every time I choose growth over bitterness...

peace over anger...

hope over fear...

I hear her voice again.

✦

I still call her Mama Del.

The quiet one.

The woman who rarely raised her voice.

But when she spoke...

you really needed to listen.

The last thing she ever told me was to go home and rest.

Maybe that's why I've spent the years since trying to build a place that finally feels like home.

✦

— Ralph.
