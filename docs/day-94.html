The Day the Clock Changed

✦

Tonight at eleven, I started the last graveyard shift. Monday, my hours move to eight in the morning. Hers still end at eight.

I only found out at ten at night. The day had started in the small hours, with Hana saying she's turning thirty in December.

The future came first. The schedule came last.

✦

At three in the morning, she checked in on me.

"just checking in… musta ka naman?"

Sleepy banter, coffee on her side. Then at four twenty-five, she said:

"Huy sumagi sa isip ko ba magtetrenta na pala talaga ako this year"

She's turning thirty on December thirty-first, and she wasn't planning anything for it. I made plans right there. December was already the date we kept coming back to. She just pinned a number on it.

"Luh ppuntahan kita. Edi di ka magisa nun."

She said she'd probably spend it alone. Peaceful, her choice. I told her that wasn't happening.

"If gusto mo lets just stay sa beach and celeb it like sunrise or sunset."

A beach on New Year's Eve, sunrise or sunset. One handa lang, tipid, and a quiet night instead of the crowd. I told her I'd watch the bookings for December. First sale, I'd grab it.

The talk ran further than the date. Bali, Batanes, a resort and a dive group in Moalboal with her as the coach. Her dreams floated out, and mine followed. New Zealand. And maybe next year, or a year after that, I move to the beach too.

Past five, the music matched the hour. Lay Me Down, five hundred miles. Songs about distance and time.

"when im hearing this song. its like im excited to go to cebu this december."

The future stopped being somewhere out there. It got a date, a beach, a sale to watch, and a move to plan. All of it before the shift even ended.

✦

Out at seven, straight to Reacher Season 4. The shift that started last night was done. The morning was mine.

The afternoon came with the new work. A welcome email, a workspace account landing next week, a kickoff, a chat platform where messages move in threads instead of walls. Setup and training, the shape of the new schedule already leaning in.

Hana woke up, and it was raining again. Lightning this time, so the errand didn't happen. She said it, and then we talked about food.

Pad thai, nilaga with bone marrow and kalabasa, kaldereta and the way every region argues about it. She has rules. No tomato in red meat.

"you really have to try my bicol express."

Chili flakes instead of sili, crunchy garlic bits, green sili never missing, gata cooked until the meat turns soft. We talked ourselves hungry. I went out to buy food.

We ate at the same time, apart. She had Eyes Wide Shut. I had Reacher. Sawsawan argued the same way kaldereta did. She dips hers separate. I mix mine in.

The December table already has its menu. She cooks, I cook my bicol express.

✦

At ten, the machine that should have logged itself out was still in from seven in the morning.

"i forgot to logout last 7am. now i have to file a certificate of attendance.. -_-"

So I dug the little reminder app out from June and rebuilt it. Autostart. The machine would keep time without me.

Then the news: next week, eight in the morning. Logout at four, no weekend shifts. One last graveyard tonight.

I wrote this:

"kinda sad that me and hana will have a different time now. when she logout at 8am thats my login time."

We've been living in the same hours. Monday, we won't be.

"well the plan is i'll look for a part time job in gy shift so i can still work with the same time with hana"

Extra income, and it feeds the December plan and the move to Moalboal next year.

"but still i need to find that part time."

Nothing is certain yet. December isn't either. The beach is a promise I'm booking around.

At eleven, I started the shift.

"shift naaaaaaa. last shift na to."

The last graveyard is running. The plan is to find more nights just like it.

The day underneath was a check-in at three in the morning, songs about time at five, a date that December got, a shift that ended at seven, bicol express we talked about until we went out to buy food, a certificate of attendance, and a schedule that changed after tonight.

The clock was changing. I just wasn't ready to leave the hours she lives in.

— Ralph.
