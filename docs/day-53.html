The Day She Knew Before October

✦

Four in the morning.

The movie was over.

And I said it.

"I'm starting to like you."

✦

The call had been ordinary before that.

Places she wants to visit when she comes.

Bukidnon.

Davao.

Samal.

Bohol.

Where someone might live permanently.

Whether to have kids.

The kind of conversation you only have when someone has started becoming part of the way you imagine the future, even if you haven't named it yet.

And somewhere in it, I realized the words weren't going to stay inside.

✦

I'd thought about it for a while.

Not the words. The timing.

I didn't want to say it through text.

I didn't want to ambush her with it in October, the first time we meet.

So I called.

And I said it the way you say a thing you can't take back.

Carefully.

And all at once.

✦

She was quiet for a moment.

Then...

"nubayan kinakabahan ako."

She was nervous too.

"I feel comfortable just expressing myself to you."

"Ang dami pang similarities like???!!! wth"

"This just makes me feel things easier."

"Maybe I have not received anything like this in a while."

I didn't ask her to say any of it.

I just told her what was true, and she told me what was true back.

✦

Then she told me something I didn't know.

That line in my bio — the one about kids, "not sure" — was part of why she matched.

She saw a stranger with a life goal she could read.

We've spent weeks finding similarities.

Same shift.

Same hobbies.

Same sleeping habits.

Turns out the first one was there from the start.

✦

"Wag tayo maakward sa isat isa pls," I said.

"Chill lang naman tayo.

I just put it out there para ung intentions ko is clear.

Walang mixed signals talaga."

I told her how it happened.

We were just looking for a friend.

Someone to talk to.

Then we found out we had the same shift.

Then somewhere along the way, I started looking forward to her.

"I did start to like you," I said.

"I was hesitant to tell it at first.

Ayoko din naman mag throw ng signals na hindi natin both maiintindihan."

She said she appreciated it.

And I meant it when I said:

"Wala naman magbabago hana."

✦

And nothing did.

That's the part I keep thinking about.

The rest of the day was proof.

"Mahirap magkalovelife ang trentahin kasi antukin na," she said, the morning after.

Beef loaf na may egg.

A trick for opening the can — initin mo lang ang lata.

Siomai, and the price went up.

She's crocheting a dress instead of buying one.

"Like what are the chances?!" she kept saying.

From hobbies to shifts to sleeping habits.

I said it's not planned.

Not fake.

Not forced.

We said good morning at four in the afternoon.

That's what our mornings look like.

Then the movie — Hi-Five.

A Korean film, comedy action.

She ate too much watching it.

"Hindi ko na namalayan ang dami ko nang kinain," she said.

She said she felt a little sick.

After that, we just talked about anything.

We didn't mention what I'd said.

It didn't need revisiting.

It was a normal ordinary day.

That's how the day went.

✦

I said it at four in the morning.

The day that followed was siomai and sleep jokes and a movie she ate too much watching.

Nothing changed.

And that was exactly the point.

I didn't need her to decide anything.

I just needed her to know.

October is still coming.

She'll just already know.

The not-knowing is still there.

It just isn't mine alone anymore.

— Ralph.
