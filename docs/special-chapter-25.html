The Stranger Who Stayed

ₚ·.°.☆•

Some shifts announce themselves.

Loud.

With reasons and timestamps.

This one didn't.

Somewhere in the middle of talking, I stopped reading her messages and started looking forward to them.

I can't tell you the exact day it happened.

I just noticed, one afternoon, that she was already part of my day.

The stranger who felt familiar had never really left.

✦

Then there was the call.

"How about I call you? Ok lang?"

I typed that and almost didn't send it.

There's something strange about asking for a stranger's voice.

But she said it was fine.

A work call tried to interrupt us before we even started.

"Timing," I joked.

We tried again anyway.

And then the shift became sound.

✦

"Sorry ang lalim ng boses ko," I said, because people keep telling me that.

She said it was fine.

Like it was the easiest thing in the world.

✦

A voice is a strange thing.

It makes a person more real.

For weeks, I'd been filling in the tone.

The laugh.

The pauses.

Then suddenly there was nothing left to fill in.

Just her, reading the same words I'd been reading.

I keep thinking about how much we assumed about each other before we ever heard each other.

And how little of it mattered once we did.

✦

She found her way into my Discord the way she found her way into everything else.

Without ceremony.

@hannucinate.

A server.

Then the music bot.

Hall & Oates from her shuffle.

A song I used to sing at videoke back in college.

She missed karaoke too, she said.

It's a small thing, sharing music.

But there's a trust in it.

You hand someone your songs and hope they don't laugh.

She didn't laugh.

She knew the song.

That felt like being understood in a language I forgot I spoke.

✦

Then the soundtrip became a movie night.

We settled on Postmortem.

A Filipino film.

She bought chips for it.

I bought snacks of my own.

We watched until past noon, then talked about it like critics.

The story lost itself somewhere in the middle.

The talent was real.

We argued about the albularyo scene like it mattered.

That's what it's like when there's nothing to prove.

Two people sharing a screen and a bag of chips past noon...

and somehow, it's enough.

✦

Now the day has a shape to it.

Good mornings after shifts.

"Kain boss."

"Eat ur lunch na posxz."

Checking that the other one actually slept.

We're both night workers.

We know what it costs to be awake when the rest of the world isn't.

Maybe that's why it's easy.

We speak the same schedule.

The same sleepy language.

We even like the same fruit.

Guyabano.

Hers.

Mine.

The kind of same that needs no explanation.

✦

We tease each other about sleeping.

About headlocks and big spoons.

About the people we follow and the ones we claim we don't.

It's not anything I need to name.

It's not romance.

Not yet.

Maybe not ever.

It's just two people who got comfortable in a room neither of us is standing in.

✦

She has plans now that leak into our conversations.

A place in Moalboal.

Near the sea.

A few minutes from white sand.

I joked about visiting Cebu.

She said sure.

I joked about moving there myself.

She keeps handing me reasons.

We talk about futures the way you talk about weather.

Lightly.

Like they might not happen.

Like they might.

✦

And then she said she might come to Manila around October.

Coffee, I asked.

Maybe Binondo, she said.

"Malayo pa naman."

She's right.

There's no bridge yet, so there's nothing to cross.

But the maybe is getting specific.

She's already picked her siomai.

Sharksfin.

Egg and pork.

I'm bringing my appetite for Beef Wonton.

We agreed on the strategy.

Single orders.

Shared at the counter.

Visit more stalls.

"Huy excited akooo."

"Kalma teh. Layo pa haha."

She'll stay with a friend, maybe five days.

If I can, I'll take VL.

She laughed.

"I was about to say you could just work remotely, you know?"

It's not a plan.

It's not a promise.

It's just the first time "someday" has a month attached to it.

✦

I wrote once that I was perfectly okay not knowing what she is to me.

I still am.

But the not-knowing got lighter.

It has a voice now.

A server.

A song we both knew.

A movie we argued about.

A fruit we both like.

A month.

✦

I'm still learning what she is to me.

Maybe that's the point.

Learning it slowly, together, without needing to name it yet.

— Ralph.
