The Day Twenty-Eight Became My Answer

✦

The morning turned into an operation.

I woke up around four in the morning and I didn't stop. Not for panic, not for spiraling. I just started moving. Every job hunting window open, the Excel tracker beside it, company research one tab over. By ten in the morning I had submitted resumes to twenty-eight job posts.

Twenty-eight. That's not a hoping number anymore. That's a system.

✦

"i submitted my resume on 28 job post. also used my job hunt excel. and research on the companies and its 10am now."

I like that I said it that plainly. No drama. No waiting for one company to decide if I'm worth it.

For a long time the whole thing was listening. Waiting for one answer. Waking up early just to check if the email came. Yesterday the whole job of the day was signing in and taking a nap. Today the whole game changed.

Today I put the doors up myself.

✦

The Excel isn't just a list. It's my own doing. Every company I researched means I know where I'm sending my story. Every row means I looked at the place before I knocked. That's the difference between begging and applying.

Find. Research. Apply. Track.

I built that with one phone and one table in the middle of a waiting period.

✦

The funniest part of the morning was the most human one.

At ten, I stopped mid-operation and typed: "now i need to eat my breakfast. hahahahaha"

Twenty-eight applications, company research, tracker updates, and the most important application of the day turned out to be breakfast.

The stomach has a stricter deadline than any company does. I laughed at myself for it. That's the Ralph I'm trying to keep. The one who does the work, then remembers he's still a person who needs to eat.

✦

Then I found the song for the day. "i like this song: Pass Sa Lowkey" by Ednil Beats and UNXPCTD.

It's a loud, proud song. The kind that says don't hide the person you're choosing. Introduce me to your mama. I want to flex you on my socials. "Kasi ang tulad ko ay 'di pang-lowkey."

The funny part is how different that is from where I actually am. I'm not loud about Hana. I'm taking it slow. Not forcing a label. Both of us focusing on our own goals first, on the same side, waiting for the right time. We are, genuinely, the opposite of a flex post.

But the core of the song still hit. It's not about being public. It's about not being ashamed.

"I'm not ashamed that I care about you."

That's the part I've been learning. A few months ago, caring felt like a wound. Now it feels like something I don't have to hide. I wrote her a letter. I told her she's in the journal. I've written about her in more days than I can count. Slowly, without ceremony, I've been telling the truth that the song yells.

✦

Maybe that's why I like it. Not the volume. The certainty.

The song believes the thing out loud. I believe it quietly. Same sentence, different tone. Pass sa lowkey, the beat says. And I keep moving one slow, honest page at a time.

The wait is still on. The NTE still hasn't answered. The applications I sent haven't written back. But I don't need them to move for me to move.

✦

That's what twenty-eight doors feel like at the end of the day.

Not a guarantee. Not noise. Just proof that I stopped giving one company the whole vote.

The NTE still hasn't answered. But I don't need its answer anymore.

Twenty-eight became mine. I hold the table now. I decide which doors exist, which ones I knock on, and which parts of myself I get to keep.

I made my breakfast, I found my song, and I stopped being ashamed of the person I care about.

That's a full day. And it's not even done yet.

— Ralph.
