The Day the Whole Job Was Signing In

✦

The wait is turning into a routine now.

Day 83 looked like almost nothing. Dota in the morning. The NTE still quiet. At eleven at night the whole job of the day was one sign-in on Sprout, then a nap. That's it. That's the whole day.

The weird part is that it felt okay.

✦

The day opened past midnight with her song from the movie night finally getting its answer.

"Wahahahhahahaha hinanap mo talaga ahhhh"

She'd gone and searched up the Endor track after we watched, Pump It Up, like the night still needed fuel. I laughed at her for finding it. Somewhere after midnight, before the day even began, the movie night was still holding on.

✦

By early morning the hello went out.

"good morningggggg"

Then the hours opened up empty in front of me.

I played Dota while the queue sat where I left it. NTE still out there. Applications still out there. Nothing wanted me today, and I didn't force it to.

✦

The waiting has a shape now. I've gotten used to it.

First the bounce. Then the applications. Then the movie that played anyway. Somewhere in there the wait stopped being a threat and became a hallway. You walk it once, you know the lights, you stop holding your breath at every door.

Today I didn't touch the resume. I didn't refresh the email into a hobby. I played and I waited and the day moved anyway.

✦

And at eleven at night, the one thing that mattered.

Sign in. Sprout. The account they kept for me through all of it, the one guard between the wages and the word AWOL. It's still a small act, but it's the act.

I told her once it was login lang, para sa sahod, back when the whole thing was new. It's still true. So I signed in, and then I took a nap.

✦

That's what a waiting day is when you've gotten good at them.

Not nothing. A rest day with one small chore at the end of it. A game in the morning, a sign-in at night, and in between a quiet that doesn't need to be filled.

The NTE still hasn't answered. The applications still haven't called. But the day's whole job was signing in, and I did that.

The rest can keep waiting.

— Ralph.
