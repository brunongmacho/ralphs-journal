The Stranger Who Felt Familiar

ₚ·.°.☆•

There are people who enter your life loudly.

You remember the exact moment they arrived.

The exact conversation.

The exact reason they became important.

Then there are people who simply appear.

No dramatic entrance.

No grand beginning.

One message.

One reply.

One conversation that somehow continues the next day.

And then the day after that.

She was on Boo.

I almost didn't open the app that night.

If I had decided to scroll a little longer somewhere else, we probably never would've spoken.

Hana felt like that.

Or maybe Julia.

Depending on which app we happened to be talking on.

The next morning, she messaged first.

"good afternoon! how's ur sleep?"

That shouldn't have meant much.

But it did.

✦

If someone asked me to describe her, I could start with the obvious things.

She's from Cebu.

She freedives.

She likes tattoos.

She writes.

She travels.

She listens to music I had never heard before and somehow makes me add it to my playlist.

All of those things would be true.

But none of them explain why I kept replying.

Because the truth is, plenty of people like those things.

What made her different wasn't what she liked.

It was how easy it felt to talk.

✦

We talked about freediving first.

Maybe because it was the most obvious thing we had in common.

There's something strange about talking to another diver.

You don't have to explain why silence feels comforting.

You don't have to explain why being underwater can sometimes feel more peaceful than being on land.

You don't have to explain why the ocean keeps calling you back.

Some people understand immediately.

She did.

We were both non-swimmers once.

For two people who eventually ended up underwater on purpose, that felt strangely funny.

✦

Then the conversation started wandering.

Music.

Movies.

I recommended Gonjiam. She countered with Midsommar.

Then Exhuma. Then The Substance.

She was into cult horror and found footage.

I had a lot of catching up to do.

She told me she normally falls asleep during romance movies.

Then admitted there was one exception: Bridget Jones.

I filed that away.

Travel.

Relationships.

The strange lessons people learn after being hurt.

The stranger lessons they learn after surviving it.

Neither of us seemed interested in pretending to be perfect.

That was refreshing.

I think I've reached a point in life where honesty is far more attractive than impressiveness.

People who tell the truth about themselves feel rare.

She felt real.

She told me she'd sworn off dating apps.

I told her I was only on that app hoping for a decent conversation.

She said the same.

That's rare — finding someone who matches your pace.

✦

One thing I noticed was how often we accidentally discovered similarities.

Not the dramatic soulmate kind.

The ordinary kind.

The kind that makes you stop for a second and laugh.

"Oh. Same."

Not because it changes your life.

Because it makes the world feel a little smaller.

A little friendlier.

We talked about the things we didn't want.

Not wanting kids.

Not wanting to pretend.

Not wanting lives that looked good on paper but felt empty.

Finding someone who just... agreed.

Same shift, too. Graveyard.

I pointed it out first.

"Hala same pala tayong sched."

She just laughed.

The kind of coincidence that doesn't mean anything on its own.

But still makes you pause.

✦

We talked about tattoos.

I showed her the idea I'd been carrying for years.

Da Vinci.

Engineering.

Music.

Geometry.

Pieces of myself connected by fine lines.

She showed me hers.

A devil's trumpet behind her ear.

A daruma doll.

The Thinker, by Kafka.

Patchwork pieces, each one carrying a different story.

She didn't collect them to match. She collected them because they meant something.

Suddenly the conversation became less about tattoos and more about identity.

About the things people choose to carry permanently.

About the stories we decide deserve skin.

I've always believed tattoos are less about art and more about memory.

They're proof that certain parts of your life mattered enough to keep.

✦

Somewhere in the middle of all those conversations, I realized something.

I wasn't trying to become anything.

I wasn't trying to impress her.

I wasn't trying to win her over.

I wasn't imagining a future.

I wasn't imagining an ending.

I was simply enjoying the conversation.

That sounds ordinary.

But after everything that happened this year, it felt surprisingly meaningful.

Because for the first time in a long while, I wasn't talking to someone because I needed something.

I was talking because I genuinely enjoyed learning who they were.

✦

There's a freedom in that.

No expectations.

No pressure.

No invisible finish line.

Just curiosity.

Just two people exchanging stories.

Sometimes that's enough.

Maybe more than enough.

I mentioned that maybe we'd dive together someday.

She replied, "sure2 sana mapadpad ka dito."

I laughed.

The conversation moved on immediately afterward.

But I still remember it.

✦

I don't know what role Hana will eventually play in my life.

Friend.

Acquaintance.

Someone I'll talk to for a few months.

Someone I'll know for years.

I honestly don't know.

And for once...

I'm perfectly okay not knowing.

Life has taught me that not every meaningful connection needs a destination.

Some people arrive to stay.

Some arrive to teach.

Some arrive simply to remind you that good conversations still exist.

✦

Maybe that's what she gave me.

A reminder.

That meeting new people doesn't have to be complicated.

That connection doesn't always arrive carrying expectations.

That curiosity can be enough reason to keep talking.

And that sometimes a stranger feels familiar not because you've met them before...

but because they make it easy to be yourself.

✦

— Ralph.
