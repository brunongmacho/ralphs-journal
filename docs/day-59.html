The Day She Told Me First

✦

Out of shift at 7 AM.

It's a holiday, so this becomes a four-day weekend. My next shift isn't until Monday, 11 PM.

Four days where nobody needs anything from me.

✦

Early this morning, almost in passing, Hana said it:

"Mukang mapapaaga pagbukod ko hahaha."

Then it got real. Two place viewings are booked for this Saturday. The Moalboal trip isn't just a break by the sea — it's a house hunt.

She finishes her shift tomorrow, packs, commutes, and will probably sleep through the entire bus ride. She admits it herself. "Yupppp huhuhu."

But she's excited. "Excited nako para bukas huhuhu."

What looked like a short vacation is actually the start of her moving out.

✦

Early that afternoon — her laundry just folded, my shift long over — she asked:

"Normal naman na magmove out ako at this age, di ba?"

I gave her the truth of my own life. 2011. Around 33. Privacy, mostly. And I had to learn how to be on my own.

My mother let me go. I visit, I bring food or money — mas may kaya sa akin ang nanay ko, anyway.

She told me her pride is high and she's scared to ask for help. "Papanindigan ko talaga to."

"Medyo same tayo dyan," I said. "Gagawan talaga ng paraan hanggang kaya."

I wasn't advising from theory.

I was handing her the same playbook I once lived.

✦

Then the harder part.

"This is my chance to be completely independent," she said. "I may sound harsh on this one but the independence I will be taking for now doesn't include anyone."

Because if someone backs her up, the whole point of leaving disappears.

I joked, "Malay mo, dumating ung time na damay na ako sa plans mo. :D"

"Huy, sabi ko haaa d ako makikipagshare muna sa sestayhan hahahhaa."

She doesn't believe in living together, either. Ring first, then sama. "Korekkk!!!" she said when I guessed it.

And here is where I found out what these past months have actually taught me.

"I mean yeah, i like you," I told her. "Pero ayaw din kita ipressure into anything right now."

"For now, i like how we are. Like chill. Safe. True. And no pressure."

Do I hope she likes me back? Of course. Magiging hypocrite ako kung hindi ko sasabihin.

Pero aun. No pressure.

"I'll support you sa gusto mo. Magiging cheerleader mo ko. ;)"

Seeking advice isn't dependence, I told her. But I'll still let you diskarte on your own.

✦

Then she said the thing that stayed with me all day.

"Ikaw lang ung properly napagsabihan ko ng plans ko."

Before her family. Before her friends. The first person properly told is me.

Not because I asked. Because I'm safe that way — the one who won't use her plans to hold her in place.

She apologized in case things develop slowly for now. Her mental load is at its heaviest.

"Wag ka mapressure satin," I told her. "Lets just take it slow."

"Dito lang ako now, Hana."

Wanting someone and giving them room turn out to be the same gesture.

✦

Music carried the rest of the day.

Sikulo, mostly. A song about going in circles — every melody leading back to the same person, replaying a memory until the world goes numb.

I wrote once that songs are rooms. You walk in. You feel it. You walk back out.

Sikulo is what it looks like before you've learned the walking out. Circling the same room. Again. Again. Again.

I recognized every corner of that room.

I just didn't stay.

Then Dilaw. Then Halik — dapat yung Tower Sessions version, kasi nandoon si Basti, and that voice has paid for what it sings. Then Ebeb, right before I went out for food and mineral water.

Some of these, I'd already sent Hana earlier that morning. "D naman mga pang relapse yan nooo???"

"Hahaha tapos na yun beh. July ung relapse."

Her own lineup lately: Oasis, Radiohead, The Cranberries. Linger before nine. <3

✦

Small things worth keeping:

Breakfast was two donuts. She caught me instantly. "Diabetic ka pa naman tas gagaganyan ka hahahhaa."

"Sorry na 😂 Kaya nga nagdrink ako ng maraming water po."

"Sorry maybe it sounds kinda intrusive lol."

"Hindi ah. I appreciate your concern po. Dont change that."

We're both hunting part-time work these days. "Pag nakarami. Or need ako. Sama mo ko hahaha," I told her.

"Pang travel natin 😂"

And before her trip: "Galingan mo sa dive ah. If you're bored sa byahe or sa accom, we can call."

✦

Tomorrow she rides a bus toward the sea to look at rooms she'll pay for with her own hands.

She wants to learn what I learned — how to stand on her own feet. I know she can. I told her exactly that.

The first step is hers.

The first telling was mine.

And me? Dito lang ako.

Cheering.

— Ralph.
