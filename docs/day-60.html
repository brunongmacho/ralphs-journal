The Day Sixty Took Its Time

✦

Two months of this today.

At 8:21 AM I was out buying bread for breakfast, body still convinced it was evening, a long weekend laid out flat in front of me. Somewhere southbound, a bus was carrying Hana toward the sea.

Milestones used to arrive loud — counted scars, survived dates, dramatic math. This one showed up as an errand and a bus schedule, and somehow that's the upgrade. You know a life refilled when the anniversary of your worst season gets marked with nothing fancier than breakfast bread.

The journal learned small things because my days grew them again.

✦

Last night she packed by indecision. Very light, she kept saying, hovering over the bag unconvinced. I voted mask, snorkel, swimsuit, towel. She informed me the trip was three days. Two nights, she corrected herself a minute later. I suggested doing laundry there instead of hauling luggage. Mockery ensued.

Then came her real itinerary, announced like a promise: pagkalapag lang raw ng gamit, byahe agad papuntang dagat.

Plans drafted at midnight always move faster than bodies do.

And somewhere between folds she looked at my old loft photos and decided — if she ever builds a house, that's the style she'd build. Midnight conversations oversize everything. I let it.

✦

Past midnight, the prep report: still home, everything ready. All I had was standard issue. Double check. Make a list. Don't forget anything. Care sounds like logistics these days.

3:52 AM: "nasa bus na pala ako huhuhu." Running two hours behind the accommodation and treating it as trivia. Aisle seat — no window for her.

"Sabihin mo kay kuya pogi na katabi mo," I offered. "Trade seats." Declined, obviously.

But here is the part that stayed with me: whatever that bus refused to show her, I get shown first. Pagkarating daw niya doon, ako una makakakita. The same woman who files her plans with me before anyone else now routes her arrival through my phone. Aisle seat on the bus. Front row seat for me.

By 4 AM I was exactly as advertised — dark room, kdrama queued, reading her location updates city by city like dispatches from an expedition.

✦

8:16 AM: "finally in my rooooommmm."

Followed instantly by: shet, nahilo ako sa byahe.

Four hours holding down nausea, on top of zero sleep after a ten-hour shift. She took meds, surveyed the room — smells old, has a pool, 1,200 a night — and reported her first meal like a crime scene: chicharon. "Ang taba ng ganto dito." Then she lay down in the middle of everything, unpacking canceled indefinitely.

The saddest sentence of her day was small: wala siya sa dagat before dark. Headache, possible vomiting, sunset forfeited.

"Bukas ka na mamasyal," I told her.

Nobody warns you about this part of freedom — day one usually looks like survival, not celebration. The water doesn't mind. Seas don't expire overnight.

✦

My own expedition peaked earlier. Somewhere past ten I went down hard and surfaced at 6:10 PM.

She rode buses and conquered rooms through pure nausea. I lost a daylight-length fight with a pillow. Two bodies filing the identical complaint: you made it to the weekend, stand down.

Rest turned out to be the actual itinerary for everyone today. We just found out at different speeds.

✦

Her last transmission before the sleep took her: imax volume na raw ng antok, baka hindi na siya makareply. My favorite exchange came right before it — I noted the bed fits two, suggested she find a cuddle for better naps. Sleepiness, she reported, was outvoting the landi.

Then quiet on both sides, all afternoon.

Sixty entries in, I notice what the journal keeps now. It used to tally damage. Today it stored a delayed bus, a room that smelled old but had a pool, a postponed sunset, and two people asleep inside the same daylight hours.

Maybe growing — her, me, this notebook — is mostly learning which things can safely be put off.

Tomorrow she walks to the water at last. It will be right where her head left it: still there, still hers, still waiting. The good things hold their ground. That's how you learn to tell them apart from everything else.

— Ralph.
