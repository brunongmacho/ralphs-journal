The Day Sixty Took Its Time

✦

Two months of this today.

At 8:21 AM I was out buying bread for breakfast, body still convinced it was evening, a long weekend stretched out in front of me. Hana was on her way to Moalboal.

Day one, this journal was where I put everything that hurt. Sixty days later, the anniversary of my worst season got marked with bread and a bus schedule. I'm keeping that trade.

✦

Her shift last night was her last before the trip, and she spent its quiet hours failing to pack.

"Di pa actually hahahaha d pako makadecide ano dadalhin ko," she admitted past two in the morning. "Like gusto ko magpack like very2 light."

"Edi mask and snorkles. Swim suit, damit at twalya lang," I voted.

"3 days naman ako dun hahahaha," she said. Then, correcting herself: "I mean 2 nights hahaha."

I suggested doing laundry there instead of hauling luggage.

By seven she already knew the itinerary. "Ano kaya una kong gagawin dun. Feel ko pagkalapag ko ng gamit byahe agad ako padagat hahaha."

"Magpahinga," I said. "Ang pahinga sa bus na? Hahahaha."

And somewhere between folds she looked at the photos of my place. "Ohhh prang loft style pala ung tinutuluyan mo." Then: "prang if ever magpapatayo akong bahay yan style rin gagawin ko." Small-hours conversations make everything bigger than it is. I let it.

✦

Late morning, one last check-in before she left: "nasa bahay pako pero nkapagprep naman na."

"Double check para wala malimutan. Make a list din," I said.

Just before noon: "nasa bus na pala ako huhuhu." Two hours behind the accommodation, unbothered: "2 hrs late sa accomm but izokay hahaha."

She drew the aisle seat. "tsaka aisle seat ako hahahaha."

"Sabihin mo kay kuya pogi na katabi mo. Trade seat," I offered. Declined, obviously.

But here is the part that stayed with me: "pagkarating ng pagkarating ko dun ikaw una mkakakita hahaha." Whatever that bus didn't show her, I see first. First her plans. Now her arrival. Everything shows up to me first.

✦

Past four in the afternoon: "finally in my rooooommmm."

Followed instantly by: "shet nahilo ako sa byaheeee huhuh."

Four hours holding down nausea, on top of zero sleep after a ten-hour shift. "ngayon lang huhuhu d naman ako hiluhin dati," she said. "pero kasi nagbyahe akong kulang sa tulog."

Her plan: "pahinga muna and take meds." Mine: "Bukas ka na mamasyal."

The room report came with a photo. Smells old, has a pool, ₱1,200 a night. Her first meal: chicharon. "kumain lng akong chicharon ang taba ng ganto dito."

"Unang kagat. Hayblad kagad," I teased.

Then a video of her lying in the middle of everything, unpacking postponed indefinitely. "sooooo as u can see d muna ako nagarrange at rumekta nang humiga hahahaha."

The saddest sentence of her day was small: "mejo sad ako d ako makapagtambay sa dagat later huhu." Headache. Maybe vomiting. No sunset.

"Sunset sana no," I said.

"nung mejo maliwanag pa sana," she said.

✦

Me? All I did was sleep. Somewhere past one I went down hard and surfaced at 6:10 PM.

She crossed an island on zero sleep. I lost an afternoon to a pillow. We both made it to the weekend anyway.

✦

Before the sleep took her, one last exchange.

"Kasya pa isang tao sa higaan mo oh," I noted. "Hanap ka kacuddle dyan para mas masarap matulog."

"ahy yan mas nangingibabaw antok ko kesa sa landi ee hahahahaha."

Then: "imax volume ko lang tong antok ko then d baka d nako makareply hahaha."

Go lang, Hana. Pahinga ka.

Then quiet on both sides, for the rest of the evening.

Sixty entries in, I notice what the journal keeps now. It used to store damage. Today it kept bread, a late bus, a room that smelled old but had a pool, and two people asleep inside the same afternoon.

Maybe that's what growing looks like. Nothing dramatic. You just fill the old notebook with better ordinary days.

Tomorrow she walks to the water at last. It will still be there, right where she left it.

— Ralph.
