Ralph's Journal — Special Chapter 30

6 min read

Hana

ₚ·.°.☆•

Some people you describe by their job.

Or their city. Or the things they own.

I never knew how to introduce her that way. I know the facts. They just never felt like the whole answer.

She is small. She laughs easily. She works the night, and that stopped being a story the moment it became her life.

This is who she is, as far as I've learned.

Maybe no one gets all of her at once. Everyone gets a piece, and the pieces add up to something you can only call by her name.

✦

The shift came first.

She logs in while the rest of the world is asleep. Patient tickets come in and she sorts them by how fast someone needs a human on the line. The urgent ones first. Somewhere, a patient is waiting at the other end.

Ten-hour shifts. The backlog piles up faster than it clears.

She studied to teach. Took the boards. Got the license. Then found out it wasn't her calling, and let it go without drama. A pivot, made quietly, the way she moves through most things.

Nursing, she said once. That would have been the practical one. Or architecture. Something with hands.

The arts were never going to be the practical answer. So she taught no one, and learned to draw instead.

✦

Drawing came from the internet.

Tutorials on YouTube. A rough sketch on paper, then a photo of it, then a tablet where she traced it line by line until it became hers.

Her favorite subject is Whang Od. She drew her once and gave it to her. She told me about it with her voice going soft.

She wanted, for a while, to be a manga artist. She's trying to get her spark back.

She crochets the same way. She saw a dress on Pinterest once that she wanted, and decided to make it instead of buying it. Her first dress ever, made from a picture and patience.

That's how she is with things. When she wants something, she makes it. When she can't yet, she learns.

✦

Then the water.

She was a non-swimmer once. That's the fact that still surprises me. She trained for it the way she does most things. Line work. Then a duck dive. Then, gradually, the deep.

Her first real fundive was in Moalboal.

Three people in the water. Her, an instructor, a cameraman. The corals started five feet down and the wall sloped away underneath her like she could fall into it. She panicked the whole way down.

But her breathhold kept stretching longer each time. They told her that after. Each duck, a little more.

She said the seabed breathes. That the bottom of that sea has a rhythm like a living thing, and there she was, sitting inside it.

I'd been diving longer than her. She still said she had to keep up.

That's her. Panic on the way down, stubborn on the way back up.

✦

She's been planning a move for months.

Moalboal. A small place near the town center, one ride away from the sea. Fifteen thousand a month, before electricity and the rest. She priced it, budgeted it, knows which option she's aiming for.

It was never about the house. It was about what the house meant.

Independence. Her first real one. A chance to be completely alone and call it hers.

She told me she grew up sheltered. Afraid, even at twenty-one, to be out past seven in the evening. Her mother kept a close hand on her. She started staying out late and traveling alone only in her late twenties.

Now she's picking out a loft by the sea.

The only things left, she said, are the rent date and the power station.

After that, peace of mind. That's what she calls it, and I've never heard her say it like a joke.

✦

She's careful with money in a way that surprised me.

She jokes about her two-hundred-thousand dream. Manifesting, she says, and makes it sound like a game.

Under the joke is a person who bought her first phone herself, on installment, nervous the whole way through. Her first phone she ever owned that way.

She prices the coffee outside against bringing her own. She keeps savings plans in her head, government ones and private ones, some she's decided aren't for her. She'd rather sort it out than have it sorted wrong.

Her mind is loud, she says. Scattered.

But the plan is plain.

✦

The cats come first.

She feeds them before she feeds herself. That's not an exaggeration. I've seen the order of her night.

Torta is the new one. Tortoise-shell colored, which is how she got the name. Alive, and glad of it.

There was a kitten before her. Born too soon. It lived a few days, and even that was something. That one is gone.

And before that, her first cat. When it died, she skipped school. Just didn't go, because the world didn't make sense that day.

I told her I can't do pets anymore. The heartbreak is permanent, and I've already spent mine.

She didn't argue. She just went back to feeding hers. The ones in front of her are the ones she has.

✦

She keeps her boundaries like walls, and I respect them more than most things people call open.

No live-ins. Ring before sharing a roof. No kids. Cheating ends it, full stop. People tell her she's too rigid, and she takes it for the compliment it is.

She's not used to being answered gently. She said that once, like a fact about the world she'd learned to live with. Not that she doesn't want softness. She stopped expecting it, and learned to protect herself instead.

She isolates when she's heavy. It's how she copes. She goes quiet, rides it out, comes back.

I've learned to let her. She always comes back.

✦

She posts the days, not the moments.

A gala. A trip. Not a play-by-play of being alive. She says photographs aren't what keep memories. It's the time you spent there, present, that makes you go back to it.

Present over documented. That's the closest I can get to her creed.

She notices the small days too. She read what I write about how we go through them, the mundane parts, and told me we're alike that way.

I think she's right. We both keep our days by actually being in them.

✦

I don't know her real name yet.

That's the honest part. The person I know answers to Hana. She said the rest is for when we finally meet.

Maybe that's fitting.

She hands out pieces of herself and keeps the keystone. Not from coldness. Because some things are earned, and she wants to see you know them before she lets go.

That's her.

A night worker who learned to draw. A sheltered girl who taught herself the deep. A woman packing a small room by the sea, feeding her cats first, saving her name for the day she's standing in front of me.

That's Hana.

Or the part of her I know.

The rest, she said, is for when we meet.

— Ralph.
