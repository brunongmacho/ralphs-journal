A Letter for June

✦

Dear Future Ralph,

If you're reading this, you already know how the month ends.

The entries remember the days.

This is the part I couldn't see while I was living them.

✦

I started June 24 believing healing was one big moment — the morning I'd wake up and not think about her anymore.

That morning never came.

Instead, June handed me a hundred small ones. Washing dishes. Paying bills. A K-drama making me laugh. An attendance reminder firing at 7 AM. Sleeping nine hours for the first time in a week.

None of them looked like healing.

Every single one of them was.

✦

I also believed I was someone who loses.

The pattern: everyone leaves. My mom. My grandmother. My friends. The women I loved.

June didn't break that belief.

It quietly changed what it meant.

The pattern I feared was never people leaving.

It was me refusing to stop loving.

Even the people who hurt me got forgiven before the week ended. That's not weakness. That's the whole point of who I am.

✦

The journal started as a way to keep her story.

I was afraid of forgetting — that everything we had would dissolve if I didn't hold it.

Somewhere in the middle of the month, it stopped being about holding on.

It became about setting down.

Writing everything meant my mind could finally stop replaying it. The memories didn't go anywhere. They just stopped carrying me around.

✦

There's one sentence I said to her every day for fifteen days.

"I am here. I am real. I won't go anywhere."

On June 24, it was a promise I made to someone else.

On June 30, it's the only promise I owe myself.

✦

June showed me the difference between closing a door and locking myself in a room.

I used to think they were the same thing.

I closed ours — gently, with gratitude, leaving nothing I could still say.

And somehow, the door closing is what let the world back in.

Friends I'd stopped talking to.

Music that stopped hurting.

Work that stopped being an escape and became something I could do again.

Ordinary days that used to feel empty now feel like everything.

✦

If I had to give this month a shape, it wouldn't be a heartbreak story.

It's a story about a man who asked for help.

The rest of the month was him showing up.

Day after day after day.

Writing, building, crying, laughing, cooking, sleeping, forgiving.

June didn't fix me.

June taught me I didn't need fixing.

I needed time.

I needed honesty — even the kind that hurt to write.

And I needed to keep showing up long enough to become someone I could recognize again.

✦

Future Ralph, if you ever read this on a day when you forget how you got here —

remember this.

June 24, 2026.

You asked for help.

Asking was only the first step.

What changed your life was showing up again the next day.

And the day after that.

And the day after that.

— Ralph.
