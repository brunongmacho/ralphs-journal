The Day I Found Myself on the Other Side

✦

Yesterday, life kept asking,

"Can you handle one more thing?"

Today...

it quietly forgot to.

For the first time in what felt like weeks, nothing was on fire.

No emergencies.

No heartbreak.

No unexpected messages.

No curveballs.

Just work.

And maybe...

that was exactly what I needed.

✦

The shift began the same way most of them do.

A quiet login.

A few modules.

A blank canvas waiting to become something useful.

I spent the first part of my shift designing mockup LinkedIn headers for my LinkedIn+ training.

Nothing dramatic.

Just creating.

Building instead of reacting.

At 1:23 in the morning, I finished the project.

This time, I made absolutely sure I submitted the DU Completion Form.

Yesterday's dispute had clearly left its mark.

I laughed to myself.

"Never again."

For a while, there wasn't another task waiting.

After the chaos of the previous day, having nothing urgent to do almost felt unfamiliar.

✦

By 3:35 AM, I found myself exploring my new phone instead.

Restoring apps.

Adjusting settings.

Trying to remember everything I'd had on the old one.

It struck me that I wasn't thinking about the phone I'd lost anymore.

I was learning the one in my hands.

Sometimes moving forward doesn't mean replacing what you've lost.

It means noticing what life has quietly placed in your hands.

✦

After work, I slept.

Only a few hours.

Just enough.

When I woke up, Dewy had already messaged me again.

Our conversation continued exactly where we'd left it.

Except this time...

I noticed something different.

She wasn't just talking to me.

She was asking me for help.

She wanted to leave her stressful HR job.

She wanted to become a Virtual Assistant.

Instead of immediately referring her, I told her I'd coach her first.

We'll work on her résumé.

Practice interviews.

Go through training materials together.

Help her become ready before sending her application.

While we talked, she opened up about work.

About carrying responsibilities that weren't even hers.

About dealing with management.

About employees.

About frustrations that had slowly become heavier than she wanted to admit.

Somewhere in the middle of that conversation, I realized...

I wasn't searching for advice anymore.

I was giving it.

There was a time when I was the one asking everyone else how to move forward.

Today...

someone trusted me enough to ask the same question.

I don't think she realized how much that meant to me.

I don't know if my advice will change her life.

But realizing someone believed I was capable of giving it...

quietly changed mine.

✦

She joked about wanting to quit.

I joked about millionaire mindsets.

We talked about AI automation.

Python.

Discord bots.

Japan.

Passports.

Career paths.

Gaming.

The future.

It didn't feel like one person rescuing another.

It felt like two people trying to build better lives.

Together.

Even if only as friends.

✦

At lunch, I finally sat down with a bowl of sinampalukan.

Outside, rain quietly covered the afternoon.

The soup tasted better because of it.

Or maybe it tasted better because, for once, nothing needed fixing.

No phone.

No revisions.

No disputes.

No emotional storms.

Just warm soup.

Rain against the window.

Another episode of my series waiting for me.

Sometimes peace doesn't arrive wearing fireworks.

Sometimes...

it tastes like sinampalukan.

✦

Later that evening, something unexpected happened.

I met someone new.

Her name was Eana.

We started talking on Boo before moving the conversation to Discord.

No expectations.

No pressure.

Just curiosity.

We talked about Final Fantasy.

Retro games.

Cats.

Huskies.

Greek mythology.

Serial killer documentaries.

Cooking.

Sleep schedules.

Work.

Life.

She teased me about my username.

I teased her about being easy to annoy.

We laughed more than I expected.

What surprised me wasn't that we talked for hours.

It was how effortless it felt.

No pretending.

No trying to impress.

Just two strangers discovering they had enough little things in common to keep the conversation going.

Somewhere between laughing about retro games, huskies, documentaries, and adobong manok...

I caught myself smiling without realizing it.

Not because I was hoping for something.

But because it reminded me that conversations don't always have to lead somewhere.

Sometimes they're enough exactly as they are.

Two people talking.

Laughing.

Sharing small pieces of themselves without asking for anything in return.

Before dinner, I invited her to our Discord server.

On weekends, we plan to watch movies together through voice chat and screen sharing.

If she wanted to join, she was welcome.

If she didn't...

that was okay too.

That realization surprised me.

I wasn't looking for someone to replace anyone.

I was simply making room for someone new to exist.

✦

Looking back now, I think today quietly answered a question I hadn't realized I was asking.

How do you know you've healed?

Maybe not when the sadness disappears.

Maybe not when you stop remembering.

Maybe it's when life slowly becomes about other people again.

Helping someone write a résumé.

Encouraging someone who doubts themselves.

Inviting someone new into a movie night simply because it sounded fun.

Living ordinary days without comparing them to the past.

Somewhere between surviving and simply living...

I crossed to the other side.

I didn't notice when it happened.

Until today.

✦

Today's Weight: 8.5/10

Not because something extraordinary happened.

Because today reminded me that healing doesn't only make room for happiness.

It makes room for other people.

✦

Maybe that's what healing feels like.

Not waking up one morning completely free from the past.

But waking up and discovering that your attention naturally belongs to today.

To your work.

To your friends.

To new conversations.

To warm soup on rainy afternoons.

To strangers who might become familiar.

Somewhere along the way...

I stopped measuring my days by what I had lost.

I started measuring them by what I was still able to give.

And maybe...

that's when life stopped asking me to survive.

It quietly invited me to live again.

And for the first time in a long while...

I quietly said yes.

— Ralph.
