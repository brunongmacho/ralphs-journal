The Wedding Vow

ₚ·.°.☆•

There's a vow that goes:

"May you never steal, lie, or cheat. But if you must steal, then steal away my sorrows. And if you must lie, lie with me all the nights of my life. And if you must cheat, then please cheat death, because I couldn't live a day without you."

Vows are usually said in front of a room full of people. This one reads like it was meant to be kept where nobody's watching. I understand that kind.

I kept coming back to the word home. Not the house kind. The kind you build out of a person. The kind that doesn't stop being home when things get hard. That's when it matters most.

Most promises tell you what they'll do right. This one tells you where it will put you when you fall wrong. I've fallen enough to know which kind is worth keeping.

✦

I said something huge at four in the morning. Hereditary was over. And I said it:

"I'm starting to like you."

I wasn't trying to make a vow. I was just done keeping it. That was the first sorrow I let someone take.

I didn't know that's what I was doing at the time. I just stopped being able to carry it quietly anymore. That's how the first one gets taken. Not in a dramatic trade. In the ordinary act of telling someone the truth.

"Maybe I have not received anything like this in a while."

I expected the day after to feel different. It didn't. It was siomai and beef loaf and a movie she ate too much watching. And I kept thinking about the line I wrote down that day:

"Nothing changed. And that was exactly the point."

There are sorrows I still carry. Some of them have nothing to do with her. I know what it is to miss the one who slept beside me.

"2 years. and he is always sleeping beside me. i miss him :("

The vow doesn't promise the sorrows run out. It promises you don't have to hold them alone. Stealing isn't a theft with a goodbye. It's someone sitting down beside you while you hold it.

If you must steal, then steal away my sorrows.

✦

The vow says "lie". It doesn't mean a lie. It means the nights. The ordinary ones.

Anybody can show up for the day that matters. The vow is for the ones that don't. The nights nobody writes about. The nights you come home and there's no line to remember. Those are the nights the vow is actually for.

I used to rush everything. Wanted the answer, wanted the label, wanted the certainty. I told her once:

"Dati kasi tlga mas sanay ako minamadali lahat. But pagdating sayo, ayoko madaliin eh."

"Like slowly but sure."

There's no label between us. I said it myself:

"I think we are exclusive na naman eh. No label, but we are comfortable na with each other."

October became December. The door moved, not the wanting. There's a table waiting on the other side of it. That table is already a vow. It just doesn't know it's called one yet.

I'm swearing it with her still on the other side of December. I used to think that made it less real. Now I think it makes it more. A vow made ahead of its witness still has to be kept. It just has to be kept twice. Once while you wait. Once for whatever December brings.

If you must lie, lie with me all the nights of my life.

✦

I know the difference between a vow and a plea. I spent a long time as the man who would have begged someone to stay.

"I'm not the type of person now who begs and chases someone to stay or talk to me."

I learned to stand on my own. These pages are the proof.

The older I become, the less impressed I am by promises. Not because promises are meaningless. Because they're easy. Presence isn't.

So I made myself one promise before I could promise anyone anything. The one I kept without trying:

"I won't go anywhere."

Then one day I said it to her:

"Hana. Just remember this ha. Im not going anywhere naman."

And she gave it back with the door open:

"edi okaaayyyy this is reassuring to hear. pero as I said if ever naman u change ur mind it's okay talaga as in. at least u tell me lang"

She didn't ask for the vow. She gave me a way out and waited to see if I'd take it. That's when a vow stops being a plea. It becomes a choice.

"even if we are not together. im happy on what we are right now."

✦

The last line of the vow is the one I keep coming back to. Cheat death. Because I couldn't live a day without you.

I've known endings. I used to say I'd live the hardest days over exactly the same way, knowing what waited at the end.

"Because that's what made me Ralph now."

That used to be about surviving. What I'd say now is softer and braver at once:

"if ever i know the ending and i have the power to turn back time. i'd still go and never change a thing. because thats the path i know that will lead me to her"

I'm not afraid of the ending. I've walked toward worse. The walk itself is the vow.

I used to think cheating death meant outliving things. It doesn't. It means not letting the ending run the days that come before it.

If you must cheat, then please cheat death.

But if I must, I already have a home for it.

— Ralph.
