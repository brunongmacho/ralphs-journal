The Day the Ban Didn't Reach Us

✦

The ban came while I was asleep.

I woke up past two in the afternoon and it was already on Facebook. The morning had handled itself first. Shift closed at seven, plate clean, a new game tried and gone inside an hour. The headline met the day second.

✦

The projects were done by six. The last hour was just waiting, then out ko na at seven oh two. Breakfast was pancit with a sunny side up egg, the kind of plate that says the night is really over.

Then a new game, because the morning had room. Post-apocalyptic, factory building, the genre I usually lose hours to.

"i uninstalled it. not my kind of game"

One hour and I knew. I slept around eight, and somewhere out there the ban was already moving without me.

✦

Waking up to it, the shape was clear. Discord, banned. Discord, the app where the early part of us lived. The telecoms told to block it, two weeks given for the company to open a local office. The country split into two: the ones with a VPN, and everyone else.

"it really didnt affect us unless you are not a techy savvy person"

So I checked. Cloudflare DNS was already set, DoH already on, the domain still resolving. I flushed what did not need flushing. The machine needed nothing.

Except one thing. A plugin was drawing a sticker over my profile picture.

"ok i just disabled the decor"

One toggle, and gone. The country argued about an app, and my whole afternoon's trouble was a decor sticker over my own avatar. Even the tempting shortcut came and went, a quest auto-completer, someone's one hundred seventy lines of cheat with ban risk and token-stealer written all over it. Cool, I understand it now. Shelf.

Then honest hunger.

"anyway im starving. brb ill buy some foods"

Ten pieces of kwek kwek, spicy of course.

"and my mouth is burning."

✦

The care ran toward her this morning. I was logged out at seven. She was still chasing tickets at seven oh eight, the queue blurring her eyes, and her increase would not credit until next pay. I asked if she had eaten. Told her to eat. Warned her about the acid. At 7:59 she was still going, and I told her to log out.

"out mo naaaaaaa"

At 8:37 she rested, after eating, drained from the catch-up.

"kakatapos ko lang kumain pero yeah mejo nadrain today kakahabol"

By two in the afternoon we had both woken. She blamed the rain. I blamed the exhaustion. She asked if I'd eaten lunch, I asked her back, and we agreed the shifts have been heavy lately. Two shifts left, she counted. Last two, I said. And after a check, nothing was waiting to be redone. A chill two days ahead.

She said she wanted to leave already. Almost there now, I told her. Konting tiis nalang.

Then the warning. More colleagues getting sick, more queue to chase, she might not be able to talk much later tonight. I answered with what the same industry teaches.

"no worries Hana. gets naman natin both pag busy. since same industry tayo"

I told her to eat before resting. She had eaten already; it was just early for dinner. The reels about Rene made her laugh the usual way, and I sent the job rejection email joke back at her. We parked the weekend too. Movie tayo, with the Lyca one added in.

The ban debate found us by evening. I opened it. Comedy na ung nangyaring recent bans. She agreed, then pushed further. The root is not the access, she said. Maybe start with people's mental health. Then she said the line that closed it.

"Pramis d na talaga livable dito pag mag aanak ee. Kaya gets talaga pag umaalis na mga tao dito for careers esp if magfafamily"

Not livable, she said, and I could not argue. The tax does it for me.

"Nabuhay nalang tlga tayo sa tax. Tax kasi nagtrabaho ka. Ung sahod may tax. Tapos bbili ka ng food may tax. Mag lload ka may tax. Kuryente mo may tax. Lahat may tax wahahahahaha"

We laughed the way you laugh at the thing you cannot move. By 5:55 both of us were already sleepy. I slept around seven. At ten thirty I was up for the shift, and by 11:44 the night was quiet. No task, nothing to do. She was just starting hers.

"cge cge. goodluck sa shift natin. message ka lang if kaya."

The ban made the news. The day underneath was a clean shift, an hour-long game, ten pieces of kwek kwek, a sticker turned off, and her messages all the way through. It was real. It just didn't reach us.

— Ralph.
