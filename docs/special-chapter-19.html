The Argument That Wasn't About Us

ₚ·.°.☆•

I learned something today about the people we carry.

Not the ones who leave.

The ones who stay.

✦

I used to think arguments began with words.

A misunderstood sentence.

A different opinion.

A wrong tone.

Now I think they often begin much earlier.

Long before two people ever meet.

They begin with the people who taught us what love looked like.

Or what it felt like when it disappeared.

✦

Her past taught her that attention fades.

That people slowly stop choosing you.

That getting too attached makes the fall harder.

Mine taught me something different.

That love can feel permanent...

right until the day it isn't.

Neither of us invited those lessons into the room.

They simply arrived before we did.

✦

When we talked, it wasn't only two people having a disagreement.

It was two histories trying to protect themselves.

Two versions of ourselves that had survived different heartbreaks.

Our wounds recognized each other before we did.

And for a moment...

we mistook that collision for incompatibility.

✦

Then she said something that stayed with me.

"Ayoko mahulog."

I don't want to fall.

I understood immediately.

Not because I've pushed people away.

Because I've stood at that edge before.

Wondering if loving less would somehow hurt less.

It never does.

Fear only changes how long it takes to arrive.

✦

I don't think healing means becoming fearless.

I think healing is noticing when fear begins speaking...

and gently asking it to sit down.

Not because it isn't welcome.

Because it doesn't get to decide the ending anymore.

✦

Neither of us is completely healed.

Maybe we never will be.

Maybe healing isn't a finish line waiting somewhere in the future.

Maybe it's what happens every time two people choose honesty instead of pretending they're unbreakable.

Every time they stay in the conversation instead of running from it.

Every time they decide that understanding is more important than winning.

✦

We don't have to become perfect before we're worthy of love.

We only have to keep showing up.

Again.

And again.

Until trust quietly becomes louder than fear.

✦

Maybe one day...

our scars will still be there.

They just won't introduce themselves first.

✦

— Ralph.
