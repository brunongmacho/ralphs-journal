The Day I Asked a Small Question

✦

I got off my shift at seven.

The workweek was over.

No new client, no new case, no message waiting for me.

Just me, standing at the door of a 7-Eleven, craving ice cream at seven in the morning.

The only flavor they had was cookies and cream.

Not my first choice.

But I bought it anyway.

I stood there eating it, this tiny cup of ice cream, and it felt like the whole day could just be this.

Small.

Cold.

Nothing big happened, and that was fine.

That was the point I was making to myself all week.

✦

Later I was starving but too lazy to leave the room.

The bed said stay.

My stomach said go.

Hunger won.

I went out and bought lugaw with laman and four shanghai.

I sat there eating warm rice porridge at the weirdest hour of the morning and felt nothing but spent and satisfied.

It's funny the way the body settles after a full shift and a full belly.

No lesson in it.

Just the ordinary fact that I was okay enough to feed myself properly.

That mattered more than I let on.

✦

Somewhere in the middle of the morning, I asked her something I'd been turning over for a while.

"Uhmmm baka macringe ka. Hahahahahaha. Do you think na we can have like endearment or something?"

Her reaction was immediate.

"Beee hahahahhahaha waaaag hahahahha."

"Ang tanong ahy... bakit?"

I started to explain myself.

"Wala lang. Ahmmmm ewan bkt pumasok din sa isip ko."

She said she wasn't used to any nickname or endearment anymore.

"Baka kasi wala na tumatawag sakin ng any nickname or endearment kaya ganun hahhaha."

"Idk hahahaha I think it's too forward for ppl who haven't met yet."

She had a point.

The comfort between us is real, but we haven't stood in the same room.

I appreciated that she was being careful instead of just going along.

I told her I wasn't trying to pressure her.

"Like im still am looking forward to meet you ha."

"Ayoko naman mapressure ka or what. Baka biglang may masend akong anything. Bigla ka magulat."

Then I said the truth of it:

"Sweet gesture ko palang tlga na nagagawa is writing about you."

That's the only way I know how to be soft with someone from a screen.

I write.

She reads.

The distance is what it is for now.

So I asked, she had her reasons, and I let it go.

"Natanong lang naman hahahaha."

✦

I had sent her something earlier, before any of this.

The entry I wrote the night before. Day 70, the one about the countdown.

A PDF.

She'd thought the link was Facebook at first.

"Bakit fb yang link mo? hahahaha."

"ay pdf lang yan hehe."

She read it.

"Awwwhhh lorve ittt."

Then she got quiet about something else.

"Pero it's sad no? or ako lang."

"Ang lungkot na ng ber months."

"There actually came a point na nagagalit ako pag nakakarinig ng Christmas songs hahahaha."

I laughed at that.

"ano ka nagiging grinch na? hahaha."

"oo parang hahahhaa."

And then I meant what I said next:

"Well. I hope magbago naman ung pananaw natin sa ber months now that we have like each other. There may be a gap in distance. Pero pwede padin naman tayong maging masaya."

She told me why she felt that way.

Being a breadwinner, feeling the weight of December even though she isn't one.

I said I wasn't looking forward to the decorations either.

"feeling ko kasi more work lang yan if mag dedeco pa ako."

Two people, both quietly dreading the season.

Finding out neither of them has to carry it alone this time.

✦

The morning turned.

She'd woken up groggy from the heat, brownout rolling through her area again.

She'd only had oatmeal and noodles since seven.

"Kain ka na proper meal pls."

She was planning to move to Moalboal soon.

Testing the place, checking the signal, figuring out her next step.

I told her to give me a video tour when she did.

"itour mo kagad ako ahhh."

She laughed, nervous and happy at the same time.

✦

That afternoon, I was back to waiting.

Still no match at work, still no new case.

She teased me about it.

"Sumakabilang bakod ka na hahahha."

"May experience ka naman na ee."

I told her if nothing comes by next week, I'll start applying elsewhere.

"If the pay is good. edi goooooo."

The waiting used to eat me alive.

Now it's just a Tuesday.

I fill the hours, I move my body, I answer her messages, I buy a cup of ice cream at seven in the morning.

Nothing big happened today.

But I asked her a soft question, she answered honestly, and I was okay either way.

The country is counting down to something.

I'm not.

I'm just eating cookies and cream and letting the day be small.

— Ralph.
