Eleven at Night to Seven in the Morning

ₚ·.°.☆•

For a long time my life had two times in it.

Eleven at night, and seven in the morning.

Everything else had to fit in the space above and below those two points. A meeting at five in the morning. A shift I logged into at eleven and out of at seven. A life that ran entirely in the dark, and did not care what I wanted to do with it.

The clock did not move for me. I moved around it.

That was the shape of it. Ninety-nine days, and almost all of them ran on those two hours.

I am not going to tell you it was a good season. It was not. I survived it, and I am going to describe it accurately, which is a different thing from describing it well.

✦

There was always a number, and for a long time the number was how much money was left.

I have to be honest about what that period actually was, because I have caught myself telling it bigger than it was. I did not starve for ninety-nine days. Three of them, I did not eat. The rest I ate, and the eating was arithmetic.

"Lucky Me Pancit Canton. My first real food all day."

That was the first one. I had been at my cousin's place crying hard enough to give myself a headache, and I was proud of a thing I had built that day, and I sat there and asked myself whether that was enough.

"I'm really depressed right now. Is Day 1 enough?"

So I made the number smaller, so the day would survive it. I did the math sitting on the floor.

"I woke up with coffee. And ₱160 on GCash. ₱26 a day."

Twenty-six pesos. That was the whole budget, and it was for food, and the next payday was a date I could see coming across the month. I want to be exact about what that means, because it is easy to make it sound like hardship. It was a man in an apartment doing division on a floor.

"well i'll still eat small now. like maybe 50 per day."

By the end it was not that I did not eat. It was that I had cut the day down to fit what was left, and then cut it again.

"two meals a day is what I do now."

I said that like it was a plan. It was a floor.

✦

I counted instead of feeling.

I built a file logger in the first hours of this, because I needed something to do with my hands. It timestamps messages and loops until you type exit. It worked. That was the only reason it existed.

After that there was always a number. Days to payday. Doses down, doses left. Applications out, responses not yet. A spreadsheet. Then a second one. Then a folder on my desktop with a name on it that was just the word for what I was doing.

"i submitted my resume on 28 job post. also used my job hunt excel. and research on the companies and its 10am now."

Twenty-eight by ten in the morning. Then nothing. Then a name for the failure, and I was still awake, and still looking.

I have to be honest about the sentence I like most from this stretch.

"Ung journal ko lang talaga consistent ko. It keeps me grounded kasi."

I was not consistent. I was not reliable. I was a man with a document, and the document was the only part of my life that kept its shape. That is not the same thing. I want it on the record that it is not the same thing.

✦

I did not want to feel it, so I made myself useful. I became the one who asks if everyone else has eaten. I was proud of that.

I have to put the rest of it here too, because it makes the first half worse and not better. They asked me. Somebody asked, and she was not asking to be polite.

"Kumain ka na ba?"

I answered. That is the truthful version. I answered, and the asking did not fix anything, and I did not want to be the kind of person who gets asked.

Someone said this to me, early, and she had no idea what she was handing me.

"please know that I also notice you"

It was the last line of a longer message. It has stayed with me longer than anything else anyone said to me in that stretch. I am not going to say what I did with it, because what I did with it was carry it and never say thank you.

Then my left ear went out, and a machine in a mall gave it a name.

"my hear loss was sudden and it is in the profound stage. the last sstage of hearing loss. but they said that lets hope the meds will work. but yeah im kinda depressed hearing that."

Good news. I have never been more depressed by good news. I said it out loud the way I should have been saying everything.

"i have diabetes. i have hearing loss. my teeth are not good. so many sickness.."

"would you still like me kahit bingi left side ko?"

✦

I survived it. I am not going to dress that up for you.

I did the math and I ate what the math allowed. I kept the numbers moving because the numbers were the only part of me that did. They asked if I had eaten and I said yes.

The 11-to-7 era ended without a ceremony, at 7:19 in the morning, and I felt nothing about it.

"login lang, para sa sahod."

Most of those nights, that was the whole reason. I wasn't building a life inside those hours anymore. I was keeping them so the pay would still be there in the morning. There is a difference between those two things, and I lived in it for most of a year.

I did not get where I wanted to go. I got through.

The hundredth day is still being lived, and it is not in here, and I am not going to write a line about it yet.

— Ralph.
