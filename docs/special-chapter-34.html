Every Time, I Played Support

ₚ·.°.☆•

I have played the same class since I was a kid. Priest. Cleric. Saint. Support.

The name changed every few years. The job never did. I stand behind whoever is doing the damage, I keep them alive, and I am not the one who gets the kill. Nobody drafts the healer to go first. You get picked when the party already has a shape, and you fill the hole nobody wanted. Then the tank drops, the screen goes red, and suddenly everyone is looking for you. The person picked last becomes the one the run cannot live without.

Supp tlga laro ko. I said it like it was a small fact. It was never small.

✦

I got good at it. That is the honest version, not the noble one.

Somewhere in the game it stopped being a build and started being a personality. I know what it is to be the reason the run continues. I know what it is to be tired and still be the one standing. And I know what it is to be the person everyone is honest with, because I do not flinch and I do not leave the room.

I brought that out of the game with me. I did not decide to. It just came.

One morning I found myself telling someone where it came from. She was naming a support she never got, and I matched her with my own:

"Solo child kasi ako. But my mom died nung 2013. So ung support is nawala din. I have to live on my own na din."

The healer starts the day the support disappears. There was nobody left to watch my health bar, so I became the one who watches everyone else's. The game did not teach me the role. It was just where I was standing when it finally made sense.

✦

In a life it looks like food. It looks like remembering to ask. It looks like checking how much someone owes and hearing the number and saying you will try to cover it. The belief statements. The reminders. Eat well. I'm here na. The staying on the line past the point where the conversation stopped being fun.

I named it once, plainly, and did not notice what I had done:

"Act of service naman love language ko HAHAHAHAHAHHA"

I said that because someone had fed me, and from that hour the cooking was the argument. I was not doing a favour. I was doing my part of a job I had already accepted.

That is the whole grammar. Somebody gives you something small and ordinary, and you turn it into a role you never put down.

The part nobody warns you about is what happens when the party looks away. The healer is the one who watches everyone's health bar. It is rare that somebody watches theirs.

✦

Then I went through the record of my own days, and I found the answer in the people.

There was a friend who stayed awake through the worst of a wait and pointed at rest, not quitting. "pwede naman tap out muna." When I left everything in June, he answered in three minutes: "Sige pards respetuhin ko desisyon mo pero welcome kpa din anytime syempre ingat palagi." And when the end of that month broke me open, I asked him why it hurt so much if I had done nothing wrong. He answered at one in the morning: "wala kang kasalanan dyan." Then he told me why I was the one suffering. "ikaw nag suffer pards kasi inopen mo lahat sa kanya alam nya san ka mahina pards." He did not hand me a fix. He handed me the reason the hurt was there.

There was a friend who held the door open the night I walked out of every group chat, about an hour and a half after I left: "chat mo lang ako balik ka na lang pag goods ka na." He left the light on for months. "kaya mo na nga 'to handle rohy e." "lagi ka magtitira parati sa sarili mo." Even the jokes were caretaker jokes. When I was down to my last fare, he told me to treat myself anyway: "spakol ka na lang pag ganon."

There was a friend who believed I could write before I did. "Pwede kana maging writer mars hahaha." "Pagpatuloy mo mars may talent ka hahaha." She cried over chapters I wrote, and I asked "Naluha ka no????" When I disappeared for days and finally reached out, her answer was the shape of a door that never locked: "Edi msg mo lang na.. Pwede ba tayo magusap kahit sandali lang?" And I answered the way I do: "Salamat na pinagttyagaan mo katangahan ko."

There was a friend who watched me get removed from a cohort and called it what it was: "huy true toooo hahahhaa ilang beses na rin. hardworking ka naman just the timing nalang talaga." Not a verdict. Timing.

There was someone who never tried to fix me. She allowed me to be exactly where I was. That line is mine, from a chapter I wrote about her, and I still mean it.

There was a grandmother who told me "Go home. Get some rest." There was a friend who offered herself as somewhere to put everything down: "Dito lang naman ako. Batuhan mo nalang pag madami na." There was a guildmate who trusted the healer exactly the way the game wants: "ah oks salamat mahalaga ma dungeon haha." There was someone who read this whole journal and said it back to me.

There is someone now whose messages were the one thing that ran smooth through a week where everything else failed. The week my own health bar went red, she was the one watching it. "pero may malapit naman na clinic jan?" "Ayaw mo muna magclinic?" The clinic charged and the medicine cost, and she did the math for me: "Mababawi naman yan pero ung untreated hearing nopeee." Money comes back. Hearing does not. "Bsta prio na ung health yan pa naman puhunan natin."

The morning it happened, I told her where the healer came from. Then my own checkup called: "Wait lang beee ahhh. Eto na iccheckup na ako. Update kita." She said sige2. And while she was checking my ear, I was still checking her plate: "dont forget to eat lunch ah. :)" "eat well hana." "wala ka pa kinakain since kanina."

The healer watches everyone's health bar. It is rare that somebody watches theirs. That week, somebody watched mine. Counting her meals is still the job. I do not mind.

I made a list of the people who held my line. It got long. That surprised me.

✦

Then the list found its oldest name.

My father asks if I have eaten. Thirty-five times in one hundred thirty days. "Boss kumain kn." "Kumainkn." "Psst kumain kn po bah." One day in January he named back the three things that were in a dish I told him about. He had been listening the whole time. When I lost my job in September, he did not send comfort. He sent a job board. "Ok. Just maniwala ka sa sarili mo." "Kaya mo yang pag subok or shit happens." Then at one thirty in the morning he came back with a note and photos of my own certificates, no ask attached: "Slmt sa above. Na ok ka. Cgi pasa more resume, nabasa mo yong chat gpt…"

The thread between us ends and starts without warning. It stopped on a food check. It answered me once with the same check: "Dumadalaw kb sapuntod ng nanay lizamo." I told him the truth: "Opo. Saka may binabayaran din ako monthly para imaintain ung puntod." Then it went silent for seventy-one days. I sent a single "Kamusta?" that went unanswered. Fifty-six more days. He came back with a flood of messages and this: "reply k nmn." That word was the whole argument, and neither of us ever said it out loud.

The support runs both ways if you look at it straight. My nephew lent me two hundred pesos three times in three weeks, and I wrote "Life saver" like it was the whole point.

And there was one more. When I was falling apart and had not said a word about it, her message was the same shape as mine: "Kohi if u need to vent out anything, nandito ko ha." She had owed money on her phone, borrowed against it at a punishing rate, and I had asked "Magkano yung need mo bayaran?" and answered my own offer with "Im here. I'll try something." I was the one offering to cover it. And she still turned around and held the line for me.

✦

There is one more I have to tell the truth about.

She promised permanence once, and she meant it:

"araw araw ko din ipapaalala sayo na nandito lang ako and wont go anywhere. kahit pa talikuran ka na ng lahat. i'll still here"

And then the staying:

"I won't leave.. Kahit ano mangyari hon, good or bad. Kasama mo na ko. Tandaan mo na pag may problema ka may karamay ka na okay? Sasamahan kita sa lahat hon."

I asked the question: "hon? can you be my girlfriend, partner in life. my ride or die?" She answered back: "I promise po na magiging honest ako sayo at all times. 100% committed to you."

Both promises were hers. I asked my question, and she met it with a commitment of her own. The record shows we meant it, every day we were acquainted.

Then it ended, and it ended the way people end things when they have already left. I reached for her through the last night. Four "hon?" across six hours. Three calls that lasted a minute. A line about the day we never got to talk about: "lumipas na ung araw. hindi padin natin napagusapan." Then a call at 7:45 in the morning that went nowhere. The last entry in that file is my ring that never picked up.

That same morning I told a friend the plain truth: "Wala na kami as in ngaun lang." A friend asked "Siya nakipag break?" "Oo." I added the only thing left: "Nirespeto ko nalang."

I respected it. That is the one part of the support role nobody teaches you. When the party ends, the healer does not beg to be retaken. You stay behind the damage that used to be the front line, and you let the team go.

✦

So this is what the record showed me.

I have spent my life being the one behind the damage, keeping everyone else alive, counting everyone else's meals. I thought that meant nobody was counting mine.

The record says otherwise. The father who counts my meals. The friends who held the door open, who named the hurt, who believed I could write, who called it timing and not verdict. The grandmother. The wall. The two hundred pesos. The message shaped exactly like the ones I send. Even the one who left: while we were acquainted, she promised me permanence and accompaniment, and I asked my question, and both of us meant it.

Some of them left. I still appreciate what they did while we were acquainted. The field was never empty.

I have always been the one who keeps the party alive. It took reading my own days back to see that my party kept me alive too.

— Ralph.
