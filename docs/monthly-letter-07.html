A Letter for July

✦

Dear Future Ralph,

If June was the month I learned to be alone with myself,

July was the month everyone else arrived.

The pages stopped being only mine.

Dewy asked if I was okay, and instead of explaining, I sent her my journal. Yosh was having a difficult day, and I listened instead of fixing. Eana read the journal and stepped inside without rearranging the furniture. Hana said she'd read it slowly, one entry at a time. Yui cried in the chapter about forgiving yourself — because it sounded like her own life.

I stopped telling people my story.

The journal quietly started telling it for me.

✦

Early in the month, I met the person I'd almost forgotten.

The math kid. The guitarist. The singer.

He never disappeared.

I simply stopped looking for him.

And then the writing came — eight chapters in a handful of days.

Letters to my mom, in case heaven has a mailbox.

A thank you to the friends who never let me disappear.

A thank you to the world, which never stopped giving — I had only forgotten how to receive.

To courting — which was never supposed to end.

To the people I love — who shouldn't have to wonder where they stand.

To the pets who loved me without asking why.

To the version of myself I'd spent years punishing.

To love itself — which I finally understood doesn't end when the relationship does.

None of those chapters were written to grieve.

They were written to finish.

✦

Early in the month, the past quietly introduced itself again.

Twelve years after she'd walked away, my second ex sent a message that said only "RB."

She needed help. Not from me — from peace.

A year ago, that message would have stolen my night.

That night, I helped her slow down, wished her well, and went back to my evening.

The past doesn't always arrive loudly.

Sometimes it just checks whether you're still there.

I was. And it mattered less than it used to.

✦

Somewhere in those same days, the world got bigger.

I talked to strangers like they were people — because they were.

Not every conversation needs to become forever.

Some are simply proof that the world is larger than your own memories.

One arrived during the quiet, when the rain had stopped but the ground was still wet. We watched movies over voice chat until it became the most ordinary part of my week.

And then one afternoon, a conversation found me instead of the other way around. It started with a message that said only "good afternoon! how's ur sleep?" — and we ended up talking about the sea, tattoos, and everything we didn't want out of life. She felt like a stranger I'd known for years. I didn't need the conversation to become anything.

I wasn't chasing anything anymore.

The entries got shorter that week.

I didn't need as many words, because there was nothing left to survive.

On a quiet Tuesday, another conversation started — we met on Boo, moved to voice, and talked until almost midnight. She said she hoped we'd keep talking for a long while. I felt the same.

I didn't know it then, but that conversation would eventually ask me to make some of the hardest decisions of the month.

✦

And somewhere in that, I became someone's quiet place.

Not because I had answers.

Because I finally had enough peace to listen.

Yosh needed someone to hear her, so I heard her. I reminded her to eat.

Dewy kept doubting herself, so I became the one who believed in her.

Yui broke down on the phone one afternoon. I didn't do much. I stayed on the line.

I didn't fix any of them.

I just didn't leave.

And then life started giving back.

"You've been matched."

I read the email three times. Then I told the person who'd been rooting for me first.

A month earlier, I was documenting loss.

That week, I was surrounded by connection.

✦

Then July asked me to decide things I'd never decided before.

A room. A reservation fee. A move-in date. A tattoo I'd imagined for years — I finally asked.

I remember the exact moment it stopped being a plan. That day, I told my cousin I was moving out — and once I said it out loud to someone else, it became real. Not a list I was completing. A life I was starting.

And then, that night, a sentence I didn't plan:

"Move in with me."

I said it with my past sitting quietly in the room.

That was the month's real miracle. Not that she said it back. Not that she didn't say no. That I could say it at all — without my history speaking first.

✦

But the past doesn't stay quiet just because you ask it to.

In the middle of July, it knocked again.

Louder this time.

A photograph. A hand in someone else's. My best friend's.

I cried.

I wondered if every page I'd written was a lie.

And then I did what I'd learned to do on hard days: I reached for the people still standing beside me. None of them tried to fix me. They just stayed in the conversation.

The past was loud that day.

It just wasn't the loudest voice anymore.

✦

We argued too.

Two histories colliding in one conversation. Her past taught her that attention fades. Mine taught me that love can end without warning. Neither of us was fighting the other. We were fighting people who weren't in the room.

Then she told me things I wish nobody ever had to carry.

I stopped searching for the perfect words.

There weren't any.

So I just stayed.

"I'm here."

"Kakampi mo po ako."

Maybe that's what love really is — not never arguing. Choosing not to leave after you do.

✦

I asked Yui to move in with me.

Then, a week later, I let my heart rest.

I need you to understand, Future Ralph — both of those were true. The same heart said both. Neither one was a mistake.

July taught me the difference between being able to love and being ready to build a life around it.

I could love. I proved it. I asked someone to share a room I'd barely finished furnishing.

But loving someone should never mean abandoning the life I fought for.

So I chose the life. Not out of fear. Out of clarity.

I didn't stop believing in love.

I just stopped believing I had to lose myself to find it.

✦

And then there was the apartment.

I walked in with boxes and a whole life in them.

First, it taught me I could be alone.

Then, that I could be peaceful.

One day I forgot the journal entirely — the first time in thirty-four days.

Then, one quiet morning with nothing to do, it showed me the old versions of me don't fit in this room anymore.

I reached for a cigarette after six years.

The person who used to smoke it wasn't there anymore.

That's not loss, Future Ralph. That's the whole point.

June's silence was a question:

Can I survive this?

July's silence is an answer:

You already did.

Now what?

✦

I don't know what August will ask of me.

I only know what July gave me.

Eight chapters that finished what June started.

People who read my pages and found themselves in them.

A sentence I said with a full heart and no past attached.

"Move in with me."

An argument that ended with both of us still in the room.

A home where every memory is still unwritten.

And a quiet morning where I finally sat with everything I'd been keeping busy.

None of it arrived as one defining moment.

It happened quietly.

One conversation at a time.

One decision at a time.

One ordinary day at a time.

Until one evening I looked back...

and realized something had changed.

I wasn't becoming someone new.

I was becoming someone who chose his own life.

That was July.

— Ralph.
