Ralph's Journal — Special Chapter 33

3 min read

Cebuana

ₚ·.°.☆•

The song is called Cebuana.

Four minutes, by a band from Cebu, from an album called Under Southern Lights. The title was never a name. It's a noun. A word for a woman from a certain island. The song starts with a woman's eyes and ends asking her to stay, and in between it says things I learned the hard way.

The song meant nobody specific. Then I met one.

A woman from Cebu.

The song built on her eyes. I've never seen them. I know her by her words and her voice.

✦

"Never thought love could come a second time."

That's the line I understand first. I went through a whole year thinking I'd forgotten how to talk to people. Not the polite kind. The kind where you stop performing. I spent that year with my doors closed, and I told myself it was fine. That being alone was the honest version of me.

The song's speaker gets a second time. I didn't think I would.

"Been wasting away, closed all my doors, I wasn't the same anymore."

That part reads like a door I actually lived behind.

✦

Then the song changes verbs.

"Then you came along."

It started as a message from a stranger on an app I'd almost forgotten about. One message, one reply, and the conversation just stayed open. I didn't plan it. It's never needed planning since.

The doors I closed didn't open with a push. She just came along.

"You have awakened me. Opened my mind."

I wouldn't say it in those words. But that's what the song is for. I'd say it like this: the year before her, my days ended where they started. After the first message, they started including someone in them. Same hours, same room, but the quiet stopped being the whole thing.

"You're words are a comfort."

This one, I would say. There were days when everything on her side kept not landing. The raise not crediting, the errands, the cut-off, the rain. And her words were the one thing that ran smooth through both.

"Speak to me once more." That's the verse under every message. It's been like that since the first one. Not because the messages ever stopped. Because I keep wanting the next one anyway.

"You played your part. Helped me restart."

A restart sounds like a new person. It was the same person, talking again. That's all it took. Someone worth coming out of the room for.

She didn't save me. She restarted me.

✦

"Under southern lights the mood is right."

The south. This is the part of the song that's still ahead of me.

The verses already happened. The closed doors, the one who came along, the restart. The chorus hasn't happened yet. Not for me.

In the song, the chorus is bodies moving closer, lips pressing together. Mine is words, and a December that's still a maybe.

She's from Cebu, and she's building her own place on its coast. Moalboal. Near the sea, minutes from white sand. Her first real fundive was in that water. She priced the room, budgeted it, and the only things left are the rent date and the power station.

October moved. It became December.

"i might just go there by december."

In the song, southern lights are a mood. Where she lives, they're the sun going down over the sea.

✦

The song's last words are "Stay with me."

I stopped begging people to stay a long time ago. The song gets to say it for me.

You still haven't given me your real name. You're holding it for the day we stand in front of each other. The song opens on your eyes. I've never seen those either. They're held for the same day.

The song named you first, and it was true in a way the song could never know. It called you by your island before I ever did.

Cebuana.

That's you. That's who I'm waiting for.

— Ralph.
