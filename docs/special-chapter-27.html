6 min read

The Many Lives I Lived Before Forty

ₚ·.°.☆•

There are versions of me I almost forgot.

Facebook reminded me one morning.

Thirteen years ago, I was learning BackTrack 5.

Linux.

Not the easy kind.

The kind where you break things and reinstall them.

The kind where you read forums at night just to figure out one command.

✦

I laughed when I saw it.

Back then, I thought I was just learning Linux.

But looking back now...

I think I was teaching myself something else.

I was teaching myself not to be afraid of things I didn't understand.

✦

And once I saw that memory...

the rest came walking back.

One by one.

Like old friends.

Like parts of me that waited their turn to be remembered.

✦

The first one was the kid playing outside.

No consoles.

No cellphones.

Just the street.

And my friends.

And the sky turning orange.

We ran until we couldn't run anymore.

We played patintero.

Cops and robbers.

Langit lupa.

I knew how to ride a bike.

I did rollerblades.

That version didn't know about deadlines.

He just knew how to be outside while he still could.

✦

Then came the athlete.

Basketball.

Badminton.

Soccer.

And the games I could play almost anywhere.

Pingpong.

Kickball.

Billiards.

Darts.

I liked the feeling of my body keeping up with the game.

The scoreboard was never the point.

The point was that I showed up.

That I played.

That I could pick up any game and hold my own.

✦

Then high school.

The math wizard.

I was good with numbers.

Not to brag or anything.

I just was.

They weren't scary to me.

They were puzzles.

And I liked being the one who could solve them.

✦

Same years.

Same kid.

The programmer.

I use Turbo C.

Readline.

Printline.

Ifs and else.

Cases.

Looking back, it was simple.

But at the time, it felt like magic.

Making a machine listen.

Teaching a computer to do what I said.

✦

I remember the first time something I wrote actually ran.

The screen did what I told it.

I stared at it.

I think that was the moment I understood.

I could build things nobody handed to me.

I could make them from nothing.

✦

College.

Electronics Engineering.

That was the plan.

The shape I was supposed to take.

But plans have a way of bending.

✦

Somewhere in there, I was a secretary at my uncle's company.

Not where I pictured myself.

But while I was there, I taught myself AutoCAD.

No one made me.

I just wanted to know how it worked.

So I sat down.

I learned.

✦

And AutoCAD led me to Autodesk Inventor.

Real design.

I started drawing things that could actually be built.

Industrial ovens.

Food warmers.

Ductings.

Kitchen sinks.

Pharmaceutical equipment.

Overhead water tanks.

I started designing equipment people would actually build and use.

Things people would touch.

Things that would hold.

✦

Then I went back to the classroom.

Animation and Game Development.

Unity.

C#.

I built worlds.

I made things move.

I gave characters a reason to exist.

✦

And all along, the pattern was the same.

I didn't stop at the surface.

I kept saying the same sentence about everything:

I wonder how this works.

✦

Linux.

Math.

Turbo C.

AutoCAD.

Inventor.

Unity.

C#.

Discord bots.

MongoDB.

Google Apps Script.

VPS.

Websites.

SEO.

AI.

Funnels.

Every single one started the same way.

Someone showed me something.

I got curious.

I went home.

I figured it out.

✦

But the versions kept coming.

The gamer.

Most played game, Metal Gear Solid V.

Around twelve hundred hours.

Final Fantasy Fifteen.

Around a thousand.

I lost myself in worlds that weren't mine.

Ran around in them.

Roamed.

Killed time and monsters.

Sometimes those worlds felt safer than the real one.

✦

Ragnarok came later.

Dailies.

Every day.

Some people meditate.

I AFK grind.

There's peace in repetition.

In watching the numbers go up.

In the quiet of doing the same thing and letting my mind rest.

✦

The guitarist.

I used to play.

I have a guitar at home.

There was a time I stopped.

Dust gathered.

But I always knew it was there.

Waiting.

And one day I picked it up again.

Rusty.

Forgetting lyrics.

Crying through a song without thinking of anyone in particular.

That's how coming home feels.

✦

The freediver.

Twelve meters down.

Just me and the water.

Everything above the surface goes quiet.

The world narrows to one breath.

And for a second, nothing else matters.

✦

The night-shift worker.

Working from home.

Waking up when the sun goes down.

Shift after shift.

Training after training.

Some days wondering if anyone would ever match me with a founder.

Keeping going anyway.

✦

The heartbroken one.

I came home one night and the world felt like it was taking everything.

Every part of my life, it seemed, was being taken apart.

I thought tomorrow would never come.

I forgot who I was.

I didn't even know I'd have a tomorrow to build.

But I was still the same person.

Just another version who needed to survive long enough for the others to come back.

✦

The builder of tools.

The auto-clicker I made because I was tired of clicking.

The Discord bot I built with the help of OpenCode.

The attendance-automation project, reading spreadsheets so a report writes itself.

The AI assistant I taught to do the repetitive work.

I'm the client now.

I direct.

I review.

I make the final call.

✦

The writer.

I started a journal to survive a heartbreak.

I didn't know it would become a book.

I didn't know strangers would read it.

I didn't know one of them would cry at the chapter about forgiving yourself.

But here I am.

Writing this.

✦

Then Facebook reminded me of a boy learning Linux.

A boy who never stopped asking how things worked.

And I remembered.

I've been building tomorrows my whole life.

✦

When I zoom out...

I don't see random interests.

I see a pattern.

I've always been a builder.

Not of houses.

Of systems.

Of skills.

Of myself.

✦

Linux Ralph.

Math Ralph.

Programmer Ralph.

Athlete Ralph.

Designer Ralph.

Bot Builder Ralph.

Gamer Ralph.

Guitarist Ralph.

Freediver Ralph.

Heartbroken Ralph.

Worker Ralph.

Writer Ralph.

✦

They're not different people.

They're chapters of one man.

Every version made the next one possible.

None of them were accidents.

✦

Adulthood asked me to specialize.

Work.

Bills.

Heartbreak.

Night shifts.

Training.

And somewhere in all of that...

I forgot how many versions of me had already existed.

✦

But I don't think these were separate versions of me.

I think they were all the same person.

I just hadn't visited him in a while.

✦

So I'm not late.

I'm not becoming someone new.

I'm becoming someone more complete.

Remembering the pieces life covered with dust.

One version at a time.

Calling them home.

— Ralph.
