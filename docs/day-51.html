The Day I Heard the Bridge

✦

Three forty-four in the morning.

I'm on shift.

The room is quiet, the way rooms get at 3 AM.

And I found a song.

"Bitawan," by Pattia.

Let go.

✦

It's about the space before a relationship actually ends.

The part where someone is already becoming distant.

Where you notice something change.

You ask.

You try to understand.

And you keep holding on, waiting for the other person to finally say what they've already decided.

I've lived that more than once.

It wasn't just one person.

It was all of them.

✦

The chorus asks to be let go, if it's easier that way.

But the bridge is where the song stops being gentle.

It doesn't ask for anything.

It points at the truth.

If they've already decided...

why won't they say it?

And underneath that...

the quiet ache of words that used to mean something.

The things they promised.

The things you believed.

And how everything changes when those words stop matching the way someone treats you.

✦

That's the pain the song carries.

Not a clean goodbye.

A distance you had to interpret.

Questions you were afraid to ask.

And the slow realization that you can't make someone stay by holding harder.

I've felt this before.

Several times.

Different people.

Same feeling.

But remembering isn't the same as wanting.

The song didn't send me backward.

It just said:

"I recognize this feeling."

That's not the same as still being in it.

✦

A few months ago, songs like this were wounds.

Now they're doors.

I can open one.

Look at everything inside.

And close it again.

The version of me who needed an answer would've stayed in that doorway, waiting for someone else to decide if he was still wanted.

That version isn't answering today.

There's a life on the other side now.

One that doesn't depend on one person's answer.

So the song can hurt.

And I can let it.

That's the difference.

✦

Later, work asked for something small.

A reminder about the upcoming holidays.

Kadayawan on Friday.

Ninoy Aquino Day the Friday after.

National Heroes' Day at the end of the month.

Two long weekends in August.

Days where nothing has to happen.

✦

It started with a song about letting go.

It ended with a reminder about rest.

— Ralph.
