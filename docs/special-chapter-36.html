4 min read

Seven in the Morning to Eleven at Night

ₚ·.°.☆•

I wrote the last chapter the way those ninety-nine days actually were. Ugly. Accurate.

This is the other half. Not the excuse for it. Nobody came and saved me. That has never once been how this works, and I have known it longer than most people know anything.

Somebody kept showing up. That is a smaller sentence than the one before it, and it is the true one.

✦

The honest-beautiful version should not be the flattering one, so I am not going to start by pretending the ninety-nine days had good food in them. They did not. The arithmetic in the last chapter was real and it is still real. I ate, and the eating was arithmetic. Three of them I did not eat at all.

Days later, ninety-six pesos of Hawaiian pizza.

It tasted like progress.

Not because it was good pizza. Because it was the first meal in a stretch that I did not have to calculate first. My lugaw, her pad thai, two people eating at the same hour and showing each other what they got, with photos, because that was the whole point and not the food.

Three hundred pesos arrived once, with no ask and no explanation attached to it. I have never said thank you for that.

I am counting the days somebody did something. That is all this is. The rest of the days are still in the other chapter.

✦

In the ugly one I said the document was the only part of my life that kept its shape, and that it was not the same thing as being consistent. Both of those are true and I am not walking either of them back.

"Ung journal ko lang talaga consistent ko. It keeps me grounded kasi."

What it actually bought me was once, nine and a half hours, from twelve forty-seven in the morning to nine seventeen in the morning. No dreams. No waking up in panic. No replaying a single conversation. I had put the day down and my mind trusted that the memories would still be there, so it let go of them.

That is a small thing to be grateful for. I am counting it anyway.

✦

I spent ninety-nine days making myself useful so I would not have to be looked after. I asked everyone else if they had eaten. I was proud of that. Then someone was job-hunting for me while she was job-hunting for herself, one trick at a time, and I did not have to do anything about it except take it.

She just keeps showing up.

Checks on me.

Asks if I've eaten.

That counts more than she knows.

Three in the morning, unprompted, while I was working: "just checking in. musta ka naman?"

"Well get off your phone and take ur meds immediately po."

And then the part I have not been able to put down. She noticed me noticing her.

"Thank you always. napansin mo rin ata pag nagsheshare ako enough na sakin ung may nakikinig. If it matters, d maliit na bagay un. Thank u sa pagpansin"

I carried that for the rest of those days and I never said thank you. I am saying it here. That is what this chapter is for.

✦

It got smaller. Early on the good was loud. Friends, a podcast nobody asked for, an invite at twenty to two in the morning, a match after months of nothing.

Late it was one line at a time.

"lagyan mong gulay haaa need mo yan"

Eat. Rehydrate. Goodnight.

That is not a decline. That is what it looks like when somebody keeps showing up long enough to stop making it a thing. The loud version was people who had just met me. The quiet version is the one that was still there.

I am not grateful because it was easy. It was not easy, and I wrote an entire chapter about how it was not.

Somebody kept showing up. That was not enough to fix anything. It was enough that I got through.

The hundredth day is still being lived, and it is not in here.

— Ralph.
