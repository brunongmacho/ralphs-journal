The Day the Move Became Real

✦

I fell asleep around 3 AM during training.

There was nothing urgent to do, so my body just decided for me. I woke up at 7 AM, logged out, and that was it.

A few months ago, I would've felt guilty about that.

Today, I just let it be.

✦

I spent the morning drinking Energen while doing Ragnarok dailies.

Then I told my cousin I'm moving out on the 25th.

That sentence changed something.

Until now, the apartment existed only in my head — the searching, the viewing, the reservation, the payment. It was a series of steps I was completing. Not a life I was starting.

The moment I told someone else out loud, it stopped being a plan.

It became real.

✦

The rest of the day was Yui.

We talked from morning until night. It started light — adobo preferences and candy flavors. She likes anything minty and sweet. I sent her a picture of Hawhaw. She sent me one of Potchi.

We talked about making chili garlic oil together. I told her I'd make her try it.

She told me to stop trying to make her fall. I told her I was just being honest.

I asked how fast she falls. She said that's for her to know.

I left it at that.

Just easy conversation. Nothing forced.

At some point she said thank you for not comparing her to anyone. I told her I don't do comparisons. Elaine is my past. I meant it.

✦

Then the conversation surprised even me.

She told me she's planning to leave within the week after. I asked if she was really pushing through with it. She said maybe, then quickly pivoted into a joke.

Somewhere between the jokes and the conversation, I heard myself say something I hadn't planned.

"Move in with me."

She asked if I was sure.

I told her I was.

I told her I liked her.

She didn't answer right away. There was some banter, some jokes about missing hours and time.

Then she said it back.

I smiled.

I asked if that was enough reason to move in.

She said she's leaning into it. She needs to figure things out on her end first.

That wasn't a yes.

But it wasn't a no either.

✦

The conversation didn't stop there.

It went deeper.

She told me she's agnostic. My grandmother is a pastor. We laughed about it because neither of us wanted it to be a problem.

I told her about my diabetes. About how it affects things in ways that have been difficult in the past. She said it doesn't make me less of a man.

I told her about the car accident. About my teeth. She told me she has the same issue.

We laughed about it.

Funny how two people can carry different scars and still recognize each other.

✦

Somewhere in between the heavy moments, she called me Arbee.

I liked it.

She said she was reading my journal. She's been going through it.

I didn't ask what she thought. I just let her take her time.

✦

We talked about what we'd be to each other.

She joked — roomies, playmates, snore buddies.

I told her I didn't want a situationship.

She agreed.

We left it there.

Neither of us knew exactly what to call it yet.

Neither of us seemed in a hurry to find the right word.

✦

I think what surprised me most about this day isn't the conversation itself.

It's that I asked someone to move in with me without my past speaking first.

Not once did I think about Elaine while I was talking to her.

Not once did I compare.

The past stayed quiet again.

And this time, it wasn't a coincidence.

It's becoming a pattern.

✦

Today's Weight: 8/10

Because for the first time, the move feels real. And for the first time in a long time, I asked someone to share my life — and I meant it without being afraid.

✦

Some futures don't arrive all at once.

Sometimes they begin with a reservation fee.

Sometimes they begin the moment you stop imagining a room for one...

and realize you've already started making space for someone else.

— Ralph.
