The Day One Person Knew

✦

The day started with a question about the medicine.

"can i move the steroid from 1am to morning? there is no store open today to buy food"

The answer was yes. The steroid got a clock: seven in the morning, two in the afternoon, nine at night. Always after food. Five down, eight to go when the morning settled.

✦

The fear arrived with the first dose.

"when my hearing loss become permanent.. i cant drive anymore. :("

Two minutes later I had the next card picked out. A PWD card, if it ever came to that.

Then the quiet part:

"still the thought of a permanent hearing loss really scares me. i havent told anyone in work."

"only hana."

The midnight dizziness passed after breakfast. I wondered if it was the sugar, not the tablets.

✦

Between the dose and the call, I told her about the game.

"we'll be playing as an asmodian race. and my specialty? support. cleric"

The pattern was old. Ragnarok priest, Dragon Nest saint, LordNine barehands, Dota support. Every class I ever picked was support.

"funny how i also support real life business in my work"

The founder's pack was already bought. Five days early, launch at nine tonight. The daily call ran on yesterday's leads and the book the founder handed me. I told the client about next week's appointment. They said yes.

She woke hungry with no ulam and almost took her cats' cucumber. She ordered 24 Chicken instead.

✦

I logged out at four. One Dota session later, I was too awake to sleep.

The kit came before dark: XO candy, Skyflakes, cup noodles, canned goods. The medicine would never lose to an empty pantry again.

Then she asked.

"Huy kumusta na pala ung ears mo?"

I told her the honest version. Nothing on the left side yet. Nothing when I tap it either.

"Tsaka trust the process"

"I made peace na din if ever. Worse comes to worse. Wala eh. Sudden tlga. As in walang symptoms"

The launch opened at nine. I took the last tablet and went in.

"ok drank the last med for today and now playing AION2"

Her shift was starting. I wished her luck before I left her alone with it.

"Henloooo. Mag sshift ka naaa. Goodluck sa shift mo po"

Seven down, six to go. Three doses behind, a launch ahead, and the pantry full.

Work never knew.

One person knew. By night, she was the one asking.

— Ralph.
