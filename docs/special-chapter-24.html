Like a Mushroom

ₚ·.°.☆•

My father isn't dead.

That's the first thing you should know.

He just...

isn't here.

✦

He left when I was in Grade Five.

I don't have a single memory I can point to and call "my father."

No games.

No lessons.

No stories.

No afternoons together.

Nothing.

✦

Sometimes I describe him as a mushroom.

He appears.

Stays for a little while.

Then disappears.

I don't say it to be cruel.

It's simply the most honest word I've found.

✦

He worked overseas.

Saudi.

Then Egypt.

Then...

I don't know.

The truth is, I don't know where my father is now.

✦

Sometimes he sends me a message on Facebook.

We exchange a few words.

Then the conversation disappears again.

Days.

Weeks.

Months.

I never know when the next message will come.

Or if it will come at all.

✦

If you asked me what my father gave me...

the answer would sound surprisingly small.

He inspired me to learn AutoCAD.

That's it.

That's the whole list.

✦

It's strange to think about.

Years of silence.

Years of distance.

Years of not really knowing each other.

And somehow...

the one thing he left me...

was a software that draws lines.

✦

But those lines became my career.

I drew machines.

Kitchen equipment.

Food-processing systems.

Ideas that only existed inside my head until they appeared on a screen.

A vertical lift.

A carrot cutter.

Eventually...

even a notebook wrapped in fine lines became part of my story.

Looking back now, it's strange to realize that one small suggestion quietly shaped so much of my life.

✦

My grandmother was an OFW too.

She came home.

She lived with us.

She cooked for us.

She read Bible verses to me.

Her last words were gentle.

She told me to go home and rest.

✦

My father was an OFW too.

Saudi.

Egypt.

And now...

I honestly don't know.

✦

One OFW came home.

One became a mushroom.

✦

People sometimes ask whether I miss him.

I don't know how to answer.

Missing usually begins with memories.

You remember a voice.

A habit.

A laugh.

A lesson.

Something.

I don't really have those.

So I don't know whether what I feel is missing...

or simply wondering.

✦

But I do think about him.

Every time I open AutoCAD.

Every drawing.

Every project.

Every line.

Sometimes I catch myself thinking,

"This is what he left me."

Not advice.

Not conversations.

Just...

a direction.

✦

Maybe that was his whole fatherhood.

One seed.

Planted almost by accident.

Then gone.

✦

And somehow...

it was enough to grow an entire life.

✦

— Ralph.
