A Letter for August

✦

Dear Future Ralph,

June was the month I learned to be alone with myself.

July was the month everyone else arrived.

August was the month I learned how to be home.

Not the house. The living part.

✦

I want to show you where the month started and where it ended, because the distance is the whole point.

August 1, I sat on the floor with coffee and a hundred and sixty pesos and did arithmetic for six days.

₱26 a day. That was the math of surviving until payday.

August 29, I unwrapped a diffuser I bought for my room — bamboo — so the place wouldn't just smell like food.

No arithmetic in sight.

The journal that used to store damage spent the month storing ordinary days instead.

That's the trade I keep making, and I'd make it again.

✦

Work kept testing whether I still measured myself by it.

A founder meeting that just meant meeting someone.

A client who paused everything — "This isn't about you" — and I didn't spiral. I couldn't control it, so I stopped trying to.

A bench that kept benching me. Recordings made with a voice that kept giving out.

Then, near the end, a full retrain for someone I'll only talk to next month.

And at some point I trained my own assistant to do the modules for me.

OC's basically my personal VA now. Tapos ako na ang client.

Clients come. Clients go.

The person I'm becoming still clocks in tomorrow.

✦

But the truest thing August gave me was permission to have a day.

"Maybe August is about being allowed to just... have a day."

I wrote that somewhere in the first week.

Most of the month, nothing dramatic happened — and I stopped reading that as a loss.

Nothing to do used to sound like a threat.

Now "nothing" is just a window I can spend however I want.

The guitar came back.

Songs stopped being wounds and started being doors.

When the quiet didn't speak, I answered it myself.

✦

And then — a confession.

Four in the morning. A movie just ended. And I said it out loud:

"I'm starting to like you."

I didn't say it for an answer.

I said it so she'd know before October, so I wouldn't ambush her with it the first time we met.

She was nervous too.

"nubayan kinakabahan ako."

Then she told me what was true back.

And here's the part I keep turning over:

nothing changed.

The day after was siomai and sleep jokes and a movie she ate too much watching.

I didn't need her to decide anything.

I just needed her to know.

The not-knowing is still there.

It just isn't mine alone anymore.

✦

Everything else with her was the same shape —

October stopped being a question and became a route.

Museum first. Then Binondo. Then the walls of Intramuros.

"So... it's a date? :D"

"Alright I think it indeed is hahahaha."

One night we talked like people who'd already decided, and we just haven't met yet.

"Kulang na lang talaga... mag-meet tayo."

And the day she told me her plans to move out — she told me first.

"Ikaw lang ung properly napagsabihan ko ng plans ko."

Before her family.

Before her friends.

That's the trust I keep wanting, Future Ralph. Not being chosen. Being told first.

✦

And the whole time, the past kept showing up.

Yui left. Quiet for days, and then unfriended.

The old me would've asked what he did wrong.

I asked what I should do, and the honest answer was: nothing.

My job isn't to hold the door shut.

It's to keep living on the other side of it.

My dad surfaced, the man who was never quite there.

His first line back was a question about when I'd get married.

I laughed. "Im past that. Wala na."

Then one night, the old world showed up in a dream.

She came close, like before.

And even asleep, I kept saying:

"Hey, there is your boyfriend."

I didn't want it back.

I just remembered it.

That's what told me August hadn't been a performance.

It had sunk in deep enough to hold a line I didn't even know I was still drawing.

✦

What I won't reach for: the old world.

What I'm willing to wait for: October.

Both of those are true at once, and they don't fight.

That's new.

Wanting someone and giving them room turned out to be the same gesture.

"Wag ka mapressure satin. Dito lang ako now, Hana."

That's not waiting around.

That's choosing to be steady.

I'd give a lot to have known that this early.

✦

August, you were the month of the ordinary day.

The month the body audited me with a cough and a voice that gave out —

the same voice that said "I'm starting to like you" at four in the morning.

The month I learned a room could smell like food, and that was a problem worth solving.

The month the past kept knocking, and I finally stopped answering the door.

So this is where I'm leaving you, August:

I came in at rest.

I'm leaving certain of two things.

What I won't reach for.

And what I'm willing to wait for.

That was August.

— Ralph.
