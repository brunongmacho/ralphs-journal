The Day the Tears Were Late

✦

I cried at one in the morning.

The entry had gone up eleven minutes earlier. The medicine was taken, the hopia was done. Then I read the day back in words, and the tears came.

"everything that happened just synced in right now."

After that, sleep never really came. The sun came up and I was still awake, waiting for the shift.

"havent sleep. its 6:30 am now. im just waiting for my shift because of our daily meeting"

So I got the morning ready instead. The omeprazole went in first. Breakfast at seven-thirty, sinigang and two rice. In the middle of the wait I showed the journal to someone, and she said the first two entries looked like sentences on her phone, not links. So I made them into cards. By eight, sleep had given up on me, which was fine. Eight was when things started.

✦

Her shift ended at eight. Mine started at eight. We timed the handoff like a changeover.

"Out mo naaaaa"

"In ko naaaaaa"

"Parang kung magkasama tayo. Tap lang no. Ok ako naman."

She called it a relay race and asked how I hadn't slept. I told her the truth, that I need a part-time to make the wake-up worth it.

"Sabi sayo i need part time. Sayang ung pagpupuyat ko."

"nagpapagaling ka pa naman tas d ka matutulog"

Four days earlier the different hours had felt like a loss, her logout landing exactly on my login. That morning they were just the game. Her night puts the day down, and I pick it up.

✦

The schedule had just moved on Monday. It was two days old. An email that morning said it would move again, to seven to three, once Australia turns its clocks in October. The app that remembers my hours learned daylight saving on top.

Around the same time, the founder's morning meetings ran long, so the call would start at ten-fifteen her time. She wrote AEDT, but Australia doesn't flip until October. Her ten-fifteen was still my eight-fifteen.

Eight minutes before the call, I uninstalled the games. Oxygen Not Included, the one that eats time. Ragnarok, the main one, both copies of it, and the leftovers.

The call ran long too. The steroids went in around ten-thirty, somewhere in the middle of it, because the whole morning was the meeting.

"Katapos lang ng meeting ko grabe super haba"

At twelve-forty-four I asked the air why a day shift feels longer than a night shift. No one answered, and the shift was still three hours from over.

✦

Her messages were waiting when the meeting broke. She had woken up and gone out to buy food for the cats. I said the meds were already in. She said go eat, it was lunch there.

The rest of the afternoon ran on turon and diving talk. The banter crossed a line somewhere, and she told me I made her a little shy. I said I could wait until she's the one throwing the lines. She told me she was appreciating the time, that meeting soon was part of the goals.

"Pero yeah part naman ng goals yan na magmeet soon"

"Mas nagugustuhan ko tong ganito kabagal"

She warned me her personality is an acquired taste, all talk here and dry in person. I told her that won't happen.

Four shifts, she said. Four shifts left until she moves. I said I'd sleep when she sleeps.

"Sabayan kita mag sleep"

"Wala pa ako tulog simula 10pm"

She said please, sleep. I said opo.

✦

I woke up around half past seven in the evening. I had eaten at three and I still felt full, but the dose had its own clock, so I took it.

At her midnight I greeted her like it was morning. For her it was.

"Next week pala 7-3 ako. Dst sa sydney"

She asked how I was still awake again. I said I'd slept. I just woke up for the meds, and the one o'clock dose was still ahead of me.

Everything arrived a beat late that day. The tears at one in the morning. The medicine at ten-thirty. The news at her midnight. All except the handoff at eight. That one was on time.

— Ralph.
