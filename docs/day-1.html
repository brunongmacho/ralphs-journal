The Day I Started Building Again

✦

I asked for help today. That's the first thing I need to remember.

I woke up to the breakup. Raw, fresh, the kind of hurt that makes your chest feel hollow before you even open your eyes. But instead of lying there, I opened my notes and said it out loud: "I recently was in a breakup and I want to learn new skills to make myself busy."

I wrote out six options for myself. Automation. CAD Design Upgrade. Programming. Content Creation. Game Development. A 90-Day Transformation Challenge. I picked Option 1 without hesitation. Python, Automation, Discord Bots, Business Automation. It matched everything I already was: a VA who knows business workflows, a Python learner, someone who builds things.

I gave myself a rule I wrote down: "Never use code until you can explain what each function does in plain English." I'm going to follow that.

✦

I spent hours fixing my development tooling. It was giving me a black screen every time I ran it. The cause? It was scanning WindowsApps folder and hitting permission errors. The fix: use a project-based workspace, clear the cache, don't run from C:\Users\RB. Once it worked, I set up my workspace with an `oc` shortcut. Then I installed VS Code with Python and Pylance.

By the end of it, I had a working development environment. Python basics learned in a single session. Variables, loops, functions, files, JSON, datetime. I built a File Logger that timestamps messages and loops until you type "exit," then shows the last three entries. It worked. My first real project in this new chapter.

✦

But then, in the middle of sharing my progress, it hit me.

"I'm really depressed right now. Is Day 1 enough?"

The words came out before I could stop them. "Am I doing great?" I asked myself. I meant with depression. But I realized functioning while depressed is harder than normal progress, and I was still building structure while my brain was fighting itself. That matters.

Then the crying started. I was at my cousin's place, trying to hold it together, failing. The headache came. I hadn't eaten all day. I wanted to drink, just to make it stop. I talked myself through it. I knew alcohol only pauses the pain, it comes back stronger. I gave myself the twenty-minute-delay rule. I remembered depression makes everything feel 3-5 times harder. I told myself consistency at low emotional cost beats intensity every time.

So I made myself sit with the full story.

✦

Her name was Elaine. We met through a Discord server started by a dating-app match with my best friend. He told her about me and my past heartbreaks; later, when things turned sour with her, he used those same pasts against me. Still, we grew close. I met her family, her three kids. Her daughter kissed my cheek and called me daddy. But the rumors got to her, and she ended things.

I forgave her. I kept my promise to never hurt her. I was civil.

She mattered to me. I wanted a future with her.

I asked myself if the deeper wound wasn't losing Elaine, but that someone who knew my history weaponized it. That the people who knew me best believed the worst version of me. I hadn't thought about it that way before.

✦

"I still chose her happiness vs mine."

That's what I asked myself when I wondered why I forgave her so easily. Because even at the end, I wanted her to be okay more than I wanted to be right.

And then something landed that stopped me: "The love you gave was not wasted." The relationship ended, but the sincerity was real. "The outcome does not erase the sincerity." The love I gave still counts. It doesn't lose its value just because the other person walked away.

Something else came with it too: "Some plans will still bring out the best in me, even if she's the one who brought it out." The growth was always mine. She just gave it a place to grow.

✦

I cried until I couldn't anymore. Then I slept. When I woke up, the headache was gone. The tears had stopped. I was calmer. Still sad, deeply sad, but the wave had passed.

I ate. Lucky Me Pancit Canton. My first real food all day.

I showed up for my night shift at 11 PM. I found a K-drama, Hospital Playlist. Warm, comforting, friendships. I tried watching but couldn't focus. Sitting here now, typing this, working through my tasks. The grief is still here. It's sitting quietly in the corner of the room, not screaming anymore. I reminded myself: "Healing isn't a straight line. It's waves."

✦

I thought about the words I said to Elaine every day during our 15 days together. "I am here. I am real. I won't go anywhere." I said them to reassure her, to promise her I'd never leave.

I sat with that for a moment. Then I realized those words don't only belong to her anymore. They belong to me now.

I wrote them down tonight.

✦

The Songs

Gusto Ko Sakin Ka Lang — Elaine used to tease me with this song. It made my heart flutter every time. I don't know if I can listen to it the same way now.

Eagles — I played their music during the worst of the emotional wave. It helped calm me down.

✦

The Friends

My cousin was there in the room, but I couldn't bring myself to talk. I sat there holding it in while my chest was breaking. I told myself it's okay to say "I'm not okay right now. I just need company." I didn't say it tonight. But maybe next time I will.

✦

Closing

Day 1 was survival. Pure and simple. I didn't build anything impressive. I didn't make a breakthrough. I just made it through.

I asked for help, ate, showed up for work, and didn't drink. Small things, but they count.

I'm still here. And tonight, that's enough.

— Ralph.
