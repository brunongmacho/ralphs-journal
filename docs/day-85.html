The Day I Was Worth More

✦

At 10:55 in the morning, the answer finally came.

Not the answer I'd braced for. I've been carrying the worst case in my chest for a week. Waiting. Checking. Signing in. Watching the queue I couldn't see. And when the email finally landed, it said the word I hadn't allowed myself to hope for.

A written warning.

Not termination. Not dismissal. They held the door open a crack and told me to come back in.

✦

"i got a reply from the HR:"

The letter is dated September 16. It walks through the Notice to Explain from September 10, the retraining that didn't land, the day 14 and day 16 and day 17 scores all sitting there in black and white. And then the line that let me breathe:

"the management has decided that your actions warrant a WRITTEN WARNING."

They're keeping me. Written warning on record. Account reactivated. They even sent a temporary password so I can log back into my Doneverse Gmail and not miss the next steps.

I downloaded the notice at 10:50. By 10:53, I'd signed it.

✦

But the letter didn't just let go of me easy. There's a sting buried in it.

The training team noted that my explanation didn't address the quality concerns about the outputs. That it mainly acknowledged the lapses. One sentence from people who read my best defense and said: that's not a defense, that's a nod.

That part stings. It means the work wasn't a misunderstanding. It was a judgment about what I produce.

I sat with that.

✦

And then I looked at my own numbers.

The pay here is lower than the jobs I'm already sending resumes to. Yesterday twenty-eight doors of my own. Today their verdict. So I asked it straight, still deciding it out loud: do I keep this and keep looking, or do I leave?

Not quitting. That's not the move. Keep whatever safety net I have, keep the income, keep logging in. But keep looking.

Because here's the thing I finally said out loud.

I want a much more stable job that pays higher. For my skillset now, I think I am worth that in minimum.

Not hoping it. Saying it. I'm worth more than I'm being paid for right now.

✦

The right way to find out is not to ask myself if I'm worth the number. It's to ask which jobs will actually pay me it for the skills I already have.

Not the generic VA. Not the person who performs tasks. The one who solves problems and runs systems. Admin, operations, Discord automation, Python, Apps Script workflows, WordPress, SEO, CAD under the hood. A combination that isn't usually in one file.

So I built the resume job on purpose. And this part I'm keeping honest:

"can you make a prompt that would not fake my resume but will tailor it to be good in my skillsets now. i'll paste it for OC"

A rule went into the prompt, and it's the rule I mean to live by: presenting the truth better, never making the truth bigger. No invented metrics. No inflated titles. Nothing I can't defend in an interview.

If I'm worth more than I've been offered, I want it to hold up when I shake someone's hand.

✦

So today held two endings wrapped in one.

The wait is over. The NTE crisis, at least for this incident, is done. I got the warning. I signed it. My account is open again.

And the other ending: I stopped waiting to be told what I'm worth.

They wrote my warning into their records. Fine. I've been writing my own records for months now. The journal. The tracker. The work I can point to. That's the file I'm actually betting on.

✦

By the end of the day I had a plan that did not include being small.

Keep the job. Keep the hunt. Target the roles where the skills I actually have are worth more. And build the resume that proves it without a single lie in it.

Today a written warning was the best news I'd gotten in a week.

And I used it to remember the number I should have been saying all along.

— Ralph.
