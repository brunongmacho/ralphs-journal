The Day I Was Here

✦

I woke up around nine in the evening.

Still sleepy.

I had to eat, then wait for my shift.

So I opened RO and just played.

There isn't much else to say about the surface of it.

One of those days where the only thing I asked of it was that nothing fell apart.

✦

Earlier that day, in the hours past midnight, I told her she's in the journal now.

It came out clumsy because I was nervous.

"uhm sana wag ka magalit pala ah. like nakakasama ka na sa journal ko."

She didn't miss a beat.

"oo naman hahaha go lang bsta wholesome lang ee."

"Wala naman ako ilalagay na d totoo dun."

"pag personal na hindi ko na nilalagay. for me lang un."

It felt like coming clean about something I'd been doing quietly for weeks.

All those letters.

All those pages with her name somewhere inside them.

She already knew I write.

I told her writing was my only soft gesture back when I asked about the endearment.

But knowing that I write...

and knowing that I write about her...

are two different things.

Now she knows she's part of the story.

And she said okay.

✦

Then the name came up.

I'd joked earlier about writing that she's hooked on chongkee.

She said the authorities would take her in for it.

We laughed way too hard, the way people do when a joke lands and neither of them is above it.

Then it hit me.

I'd never actually asked her real name.

"Wait. I never asked you full name. Ung totoong name."

She dodged the question at first and let the joke keep going.

But I pushed.

The way you push for something small that you want anyway.

"Hana or Hannah ganern hahaha."

"Hannah yan tingin ko talaga eh."

She gave me the short version of herself and stopped there.

"enough naman siguro first name hahaha."

"pero pramis d ako wanted or anything."

I gave her mine in full instead.

"Like sakin. RB. Kasi Ralph Bernard."

She gave me her short name.

I gave her my long one.

One small trade, one at a time, and I'm collecting these little ordinary pieces of her.

✦

That night, I went out to buy food.

Plain lugaw.

No laman.

No gulay.

Just warm rice porridge because I was hungry, it was cheap, and it was there.

She asked what I got.

"Ayaaaan tas may mga gulay2 naman?"

"Wala. Plain lugaw lang haha."

"Kumain ka na ba?"

It's a small question.

But I've learned that asking someone if they've eaten is one of the ways I say I care.

We both have that habit now.

Checking in.

Making sure the other one ate.

Making sure the other one slept.

Little things that weren't there before.

✦

She clocked out late.

Her back was hurting.

"sakit na ng likod ko."

"Sign of aging na yan beh."

She laughed it off.

Then she asked if I wanted to watch a movie later.

"gusto mo magmovie mamaya? mga noon or afternoon naa."

"Uyyy cge."

"Bibili din ako snacks pag nunuod na tayo hehe."

A movie.

Her on her screen.

Me on mine.

Snacks on both sides.

One more ordinary night that isn't empty.

One more night quietly pulling the days a little closer to October.

✦

Then, in the middle of the night, she said she needed to vent.

"sorry labas na muna ako."

"just want to vent out."

I told her to go on.

I told her I was here.

"Im here ha. Pwede ka din mag vent out dito. No judgement. I wont try fix it. Makinig lang ako."

I meant that part the most.

She said she felt like she'd be estranged soon.

That no one was talking to her where she was.

I didn't try to fix it.

I just stayed.

"Tiis nalang konti hana."

"Mag dinner ka na."

She said she'd go sleep.

I told her to rest well.

See you sa shift.

✦

And then I woke up at nine that evening still carrying all of it.

The reveal.

The name.

The movie.

The quiet in her voice.

Anyone watching my day would probably say it was nothing.

I played RO.

I ate.

I worked.

But somewhere inside the ordinary, I noticed something.

A normal evening doesn't feel empty anymore.

The country is still counting down to something.

I'm not.

I'm just here.

One ordinary night.

And somehow...

I wasn't alone in it.

— Ralph.
